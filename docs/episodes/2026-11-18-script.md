## Women's News

A viral "Saturday Night Live" sketch has turned into a national conversation about who actually takes care of aging parents. The sketch, called "Sister," features John Mulaney promoting a fake service that provides free, round-the-clock elder care, which turns out to just be his sister handling everything while he gets his life back. Leonore Carpenter, a Philadelphia-based professor, is one of the 38.2 million Americans providing unpaid care to someone over 65. She said the sketch landed hard with her and her friends because it's exactly what she's living through after her mother's health declined this year. Writers at Ms. Magazine described similar reactions, saying the sketch captured how caregiving work disproportionately falls on daughters, even when there are other siblings. November is National Family Caregivers Month, which is part of why the sketch has resonated so widely right now.

In Alaska, Democrat Mary Peltola has flipped a U.S. Senate seat and made history in the process. Peltola defeated Republican incumbent Senator Dan Sullivan in the state's ranked-choice general election, according to a Decision Desk HQ projection. She's set to become the first Alaska Native elected to the U.S. Senate. Peltola previously became the first Alaska Native elected to the U.S. House, after winning a 2022 special election, though she lost her reelection bid in 2024. Her Senate win brings Democrats to 56 seats in that chamber. The race was also clouded by controversy after a New York Times report linked Peltola's campaign to an effort to recruit a different man, also named Dan Sullivan, to run and split the vote. The Justice Department said it was suing over the matter, even though Alaska's Supreme Court had already ruled that the second Sullivan could appear on the ballot.

Also out of this month's elections, a wave of new governors is reshaping the gender balance of American statehouses. By January, fifteen states will be led by Democratic women governors, meaning two in five Americans will live under one. That's up from just two states in 2018. Democrats are on track to flip six governorships this cycle, bringing their total to thirty states, half of them led by women. Three more states, Arkansas, New Hampshire and Tennessee, will be led by Republican women governors. Massachusetts Governor Maura Healey said the results reflect voters trusting women to manage state economies and look out for their residents.

On public health, the United States has officially lost its measles elimination status, a designation it held for more than twenty-five years. The Pan American Health Organization confirmed the change, meaning the country has had continuous measles transmission for more than twelve months. More than 5,400 measles cases have been reported so far in 2026, more than double the total for all of 2025. Public health experts say the loss of elimination status won't change anything immediately, but it underscores the growing challenge of vaccinating the public amid years of misinformation about vaccines.

And a new survey published in The Journal of Sex Research finds that many teenage girls and young women are turning to unproven methods to try to prevent pregnancy after sex, rather than evidence-based options like Plan B or a copper IUD. Among more than six hundred respondents who'd had sex and weren't pregnant, about a quarter said they used some method afterward to try to prevent pregnancy. Just nine percent used something with proven effectiveness. Sixteen percent used methods with no scientific backing, including prayer, douching, or jumping up and down, pointing researchers toward gaps in access to and knowledge about effective contraception.

## the NWSL

The Houston Dash have a new head coach, and it's a familiar face from a rival sideline. The club named Seb Hines on Tuesday, just months after the Orlando Pride fired him in late August with that team sitting twelfth in the league standings. Hines took over Orlando back in 2022 as the NWSL's first Black head coach, and led that team to both the NWSL Shield and the NWSL Championship in 2024, becoming only the second coach in league history to win that domestic double. Houston fired its previous coach, Fabrice Gautrat, two weeks after Orlando moved on from Hines. Angela Hucles Mangano, the Dash's president of women's soccer, said Hines's track record of competing for championships made him the right fit to maximize the roster's potential.

Meanwhile, Racing Louisville's ownership confirmed it's looking to sell the team. The club said Tuesday that the financial demands of competing in a fast-growing league require more investment than its current ownership group can provide. That group, Soccer Holdings LLC, chaired by John Neace, bought Racing as an expansion side back in 2021 for reportedly under two million dollars. Since then, NWSL franchise values have exploded. The league's newest club, set to begin play in Columbus in 2028, cost a record expansion fee of 205 million dollars. Soccer Holdings is also reportedly nearing a deal with the owners of MLS club FC Cincinnati to relocate Racing to Cincinnati for that same 2028 season. On the field, Racing finished this season seven wins, twenty losses, and three draws, averaging around 5,500 fans a game, ahead of only the Chicago Stars in both categories.
