For anyone in Mercer County looking to beat the lines closer to Election Day, this is the window to do it.

## the WNBA

The WNBA Finals took a sharp turn over the weekend, and the series is now even heading into a pivotal Game 5.

Golden State's Valkyries shut down the Atlanta Dream in Game 4, winning 67 to 62 to tie the best-of-seven series at two games apiece. The Valkyries struggled from three-point range for much of the night, but they won the game on the margins. They outshot Atlanta 72 to 54 in field goal attempts, forced 18 turnovers, and controlled the boards. Guard Tiffany Hayes scored 13 points in the second half to hold off a late push from the Dream. Atlanta's Angel Reese was limited to just eight points. The win also means the Valkyries take home-court advantage back to San Francisco.

Game 5 tips off at 8 p.m. Eastern. If the series goes the distance, there's already a wrinkle for Game 6. The Dream are working to sort out a scheduling conflict that's left the venue and start time for that potential game still undecided.

Off the court, the WNBA named its All-WNBA teams. A'ja Wilson and Breanna Stewart headline a first team that also includes Caitlin Clark, Kelsey Mitchell, and rookie Olivia Miles, a mix of veteran stars and new blood.

Players in the Finals addressed criticism from President Trump and others over players leaving the court before the national anthem. Several said they're staying focused on the series rather than engaging with the comments.

Also in Atlanta, Dream guard Allisha Gray says she's still not fully healthy as she plays through a left hand injury during the Finals, though she described herself as feeling fine enough to continue.

Finally, the Indiana Fever are undergoing a front office shake-up. The team fired general manager Amber Cox on Friday, just hours after president Kelly Krauskopf announced she was stepping down. Despite the upheaval, sources say coach Stephanie White is expected to remain in place as Indiana looks to keep its championship ambitions intact.
