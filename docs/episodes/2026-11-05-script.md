## New Jersey Politics

Governor Mikie Sherrill spent the days after the election on offense. She's now put twelve fuel companies on notice. These are firms that operate ports or terminals in New Jersey. Sherrill told them buying or unloading Russian diesel is illegal, and warned of sanctions for anyone who does it anyway. It's a direct shot at energy firms doing business in New Jersey's ports, tying state enforcement power to a federal sanctions regime on Russia.

Sherrill also spent part of the week celebrating. She toasted what she called resounding Democratic wins in the midterms, and argued voters were sending a message about affordability and accountability. That message showed up in the numbers nationally too. Democrats are now positioned to hold a majority of the nation's governorships for the first time in sixteen years, after Republicans suffered broad losses across the country. New Jersey Monitor is also describing wide Democratic gains in Congress, with Democrats picking up comfortable majorities in both the Senate and the House, though several races were still uncalled as of Wednesday morning.

Reaction from the White House took a different tone. Vice President JD Vance said Wednesday that the Republican losses were mainly driven by the administration's war in Iran. He and President Trump both pointed to that conflict as the main reason for what New Jersey Monitor is calling a blue tsunami.

Here at home, NJ Spotlight News found that New Jersey voters were thinking about much the same thing. Polling on what's on voters' minds found broad agreement around three issues: the economy, Trump, and Iran. Not much else cracked that level of consensus.

Meanwhile, New Jersey's congressional delegation is taking shape for the new term. NJ Spotlight News has published a rundown of who will represent the state in Washington, covering both newly elected members and those returning for another term.

And advocates are pressing Trenton on a different front. They're calling on the state to hold a public hearing on how New Jersey has spent its opioid settlement money, after reports raised questions about some of that spending. Advocates want the state to open that spending up to public scrutiny rather than leave it to internal review.

## Markets

Oil is the story driving everything else this morning, so let's start there. Brent crude tumbled almost 9 percent in the prior session and is holding near a two-month low. Investors are betting that a Democratic sweep in the US midterm elections could accelerate efforts to end the conflict in the Middle East. That plunge is rippling through markets far from Washington. In India, stocks and bonds rallied, since cheaper crude eases inflation risk and takes pressure off the country's finances as a major oil importer. In the Philippines, the picture is the opposite problem in reverse. October inflation there accelerated to 7.8 percent, close to a four-year high, with the central bank now more likely to raise rates again after months of energy costs climbing due to the conflict.

That same midterm result is reshaping equity trading in the US before the opening bell. A rotation trade that started after the election is now in its second day. Money is moving out of chipmakers and into sectors seen as more sensitive to the broader economy. Ed Yardeni, president of Yardeni Research, told Bloomberg Television he sees the ten-year Treasury yield as attractive near 5 percent, with inflation expected to cool, and he's picking the Nasdaq 100 to outperform over the next two years despite the chip selloff. Over in Europe, where markets are in the middle of their trading session, that same rotation out of the AI trade is pushing stocks toward a fresh record. European shares briefly topped their previous closing high, helped along by a strong earnings season. One name working against that trend is Rheinmetall, the German defense firm, whose shares plunged after it cut its 2026 sales guidance for a second time, blaming delays in its Boxer armored vehicle program that are pushing orders into next year.

Also in Europe, two central bank stories are in focus. The Bank of England looks set to raise interest rates by a quarter point to 4 percent, its first hike in more than three years, with traders already looking ahead to what December might bring. And at the European Central Bank, Governing Council member Ante Zigman said there's what he called a rather big chance of a rate hike next month, which would be the third increase since the Iran war began.

## Tech and AI

The AI spending story took a strange turn this week, and it starts with the election results. Ars Technica reports that voters across the country backed measures to rein in data centers, with the clearest signal coming anywhere voters got a direct say. Big Tech executives are reportedly framing the backlash as anti-Trump sentiment rather than anti-tech sentiment. But The Register's analysis suggests something broader is happening. Voters in several states pushed back directly on data center expansion, regardless of party.

That pushback lands awkwardly next to this week's earnings calls. The Verge reports that four of the biggest companies in the AI race just finished quarterly calls, and the message was consistent. Spending on AI data centers is not slowing down. Neither is the gap between that spending and any actual return on it. Qualcomm, meanwhile, is leaning hard into the data center business as a hedge. The company says smartphone sales fell by around 20 percent compared to last year, so it's betting on partnerships with Amazon and Meta to make up the difference. AMD posted a record quarter too, with its data center business doubling in size, even as its gaming revenue fell 40 percent. And The Register notes on-premises server sales for AI and high-performance computing are up 20 percent this year, though it cautions that's as much about rising costs as it is about rising demand.

Not every data center story this week was about growth. The Register reports that Google suffered an outage at a London data center in October, after backup generators failed to kick in during a power loss. The cause was a bad configuration in the facility's control systems, and maintenance work happening at the time had already reduced the site's resilience.

In the robotaxi business, Uber is making its case that it, not its rivals, can turn self-driving rides into a profitable business. The Register notes the timing is pointed. Uber made that argument just hours after Waymo, the Alphabet-owned autonomous driving company, pulled its service off Uber's app.

On the corporate moves front, Meta has acquired Conductor, a startup that builds software for orchestrating AI agents. And HP has hired Emma Wilson, the former GSK executive, as its new CEO, moving from pharmaceuticals into printers and PCs.

Apple is expanding what iCloud scans. The Register reports Apple will start checking publicly shared iCloud links for what it calls harmful content, though the company hasn't defined what that term covers. Separately, Apple is finally letting developers test Siri integration with their apps, more than two years after first promising the feature.

And security researchers at Ars Technica say a group believed to have ties to Russian intelligence exploited flaws in both iOS and Android to infect phones, apparently targeting diplomatic personnel.

## the NWSL

The NWSL playoff picture is set, and it kicks off this Saturday with the San Diego Wave hosting the Utah Royals.

History says buckle up for surprises. Only three times in the last twelve NWSL seasons has the team that wins the regular season Shield gone on to also win the Championship. Anything really can happen once the bracket starts.

Not everyone made it. The North Carolina Courage, the league's most decorated franchise, fell just short of a playoff berth. So did the Denver Summit, the league's newest expansion club, in their first season. Both came close and missed out.

On Decision Day, Angel City FC put on a show, beating the Boston Legacy four to one. Nealy Martin scored her first-ever NWSL goal to open the rout, and that lead held up as Angel City pulled away.

Looking ahead, the league's next expansion wave is taking shape. Columbus and Atlanta City FC have gotten the green light on roster-building rules ahead of their planned 2028 NWSL debuts, giving both new clubs a clear path to start assembling players for their inaugural rosters.
