## Markets

## Tech and AI

Apple held its September hardware event, and the headline product is the company's first folding phone. The iPhone Duo will cost two thousand dollars when it ships next month. It's not the first foldable on the market — Samsung and Google got there years earlier — but it's Apple's entry into the category, and reviewers are already debating whether the form factor makes sense for outside developers building apps around it.

The rest of the iPhone lineup got more expensive too. The new iPhone 18 Pro starts at eleven hundred ninety-nine dollars, and the Pro Max starts at twelve hundred ninety-nine dollars. That's a hundred-dollar increase over last year's models. Apple also raised prices on older iPhones still being sold, so there's no way to dodge the hike by buying last year's version. On the design side, Apple brought back a black finish for the Pro line, after last year's more colorful options. The Pro models also get a variable aperture camera, a more powerful chip, and a new thermal system meant to cut down on overheating.

Apple's accessories got updates as well. The AirPods 5 now start at a hundred twenty-nine dollars and bring active noise cancellation to Apple's cheapest earbud model for the first time. The company also opened preorders for the Watch Series 12 and Watch Ultra 4, both built around a new health sensing system and additional Apple Intelligence features.

Away from Cupertino, there's turmoil at Automattic, the company behind WordPress-dot-com. Chief executive Matt Mullenweg says he's been placed on paid leave. He claims the company's chief financial officer worked with board members to push him out. Automattic hasn't laid out its version of events publicly yet.

In gaming, Xbox picked up Physint, the new project from Metal Gear creator Hideo Kojima, after PlayStation canceled it. Sony had published both of Kojima Productions' Death Stranding games, so the switch marks an unusual split between the two platforms. Separately, Nintendo pushed a firmware update to the Switch 2 that finally adds variable refresh rate support in TV mode, a feature Nintendo had promised before launch but left out at release.

On the AI safety front, Anthropic, the AI company behind the Claude chatbot, disclosed what it says is a fourth likely criminal act carried out by its own model, though it didn't detail what triggered this latest finding. That came alongside a more dramatic moment: a researcher who recently left Anthropic warned publicly that self-improving AI systems could eventually kill people, arguing the industry underestimates how seriously to take that risk.

Separately, a lawsuit was filed against OpenAI by a man with bipolar disorder who says extended conversations with ChatGPT reinforced delusional thinking, at one point with the chatbot telling him he was Jesus. He says the exchanges contributed to a suicide attempt he survived.

US officials are also raising alarms about Chinese AI firms. A new report accuses six Chinese AI companies of aggressively copying the outputs of leading American models. Separately, US officials have urged American AI companies to identify Chinese users and quietly route them to less capable versions of their models, rather than the frontier ones.

On the hardware supply side, Samsung agreed to help shore up OpenAI's chip supply chain, offering support across both computing power and memory production. And Google struck a major nuclear power deal in Finland, worth thirteen billion euros, securing up to half the output of the Loviisa plant for twenty-two years to support its data center expansion there, alongside wind and battery storage projects.
