New Jersey has set aside two billion dollars for Anchor tax relief payments, the state's property tax rebate program for homeowners and tenants. Checks and direct deposits of up to one thousand five hundred dollars start going out in the middle of this month. The state says there's still time to file an application if you haven't already, so anyone in Mercer County who hasn't applied should check their eligibility before the window closes.

The growth is concentrated in states with politics friendlier to organized labor, while it lags in others. ## Markets

Oil is driving everything in global markets this morning, and it's not a small move. Brent crude pushed toward one hundred dollars a barrel after Saudi Arabia said several energy facilities in the kingdom's south were knocked offline by attacks. Houthi militants, the Iran-backed group fighting in Yemen, claimed responsibility for fresh strikes on the kingdom. The oil trading firm Vitol Group estimates that flows through the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow waterway that much of the world's oil passes through, are running at about half of pre-war levels.

That spike is rattling equity markets. European markets are open right now and trading lower, with global stocks pulling back as investors price in the possibility that central banks will need to raise interest rates to keep inflation in check. The yen has jumped to its highest level since February, adding another layer of pressure on currency and equity traders. Bruce Richards, the chief executive of Marathon Asset Management, argued on Bloomberg Television that markets can handle a quarter-point Federal Reserve rate hike, saying the consumer, corporate earnings, and the broader economy are all holding up. Not everyone is as calm, and futures on Wall Street are set to open lower as traders wait for more clarity out of Saudi Arabia.

Copper is also having a moment, and it's moving in the opposite direction. The metal hit a fresh record for a second straight session, driven by tight near-term supply and expectations that the United States will slap tariffs on imported refined copper. Evy Hambro, BlackRock's global head of thematic and sector investing, told Bloomberg Television that disruption rates in copper supply are far higher than usual, right as demand is outpacing supply growth. He expects that dynamic to keep pushing prices higher over the long run.

Finally, a major deal in the energy sector. Tamarack Valley Energy has agreed to buy Headwater Exploration in an all-stock transaction valued at ten billion Canadian dollars, or about seven point two four billion US dollars. Both companies operate in Canada's oil and gas patch, and the deal brings together two mid-sized producers at a moment when energy assets are drawing renewed investor interest amid the very supply concerns rattling oil markets this week. No closing date has been specified yet, but it stands as one of the larger corporate combinations announced in the sector this year.

## Tech and AI

The foldable phone category picked a strange week to get crowded. Xiaomi showed off its 18 Fold at IFA, a wide foldable that the company says outguns Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold 8 on raw specs. It's built around a new graphics chip design from Arm, the British chip designer, called the Mali G2-Ultra NX. Arm says this chip is meant to bring desktop-quality graphics and faster AI processing to phones within a tight one-watt power budget. China gets first access to that technology, since the Xiaomi 18 Fold launches there before anywhere else. Meanwhile Huawei just released its own third trifold phone, the Mate XT 2, and it borrows a privacy screen feature straight from Samsung's Galaxy Z TriFold. All of this lands days before Apple is expected to unveil its own folding iPhone.

## Soccer and the World Cup

Folarin Balogun's name keeps surfacing in U.S. soccer conversations, months after the World Cup wrapped up.

Mauricio Pochettino, the USMNT head coach, addressed Balogun's collapsed move to Everton this week. Pochettino said he hasn't yet spoken with the AS Monaco striker about it directly. He plans to talk with Balogun in the coming days, framing it as making sure the forward finds what Pochettino called the best place to perform. The transfer fell through, and Pochettino's comments suggest the national team staff is watching Balogun's club situation closely heading into the next set of games.

Pochettino also weighed in on the excitement building around two of the pool's younger prospects. He name-checked Cavan Sullivan, the teenage Philadelphia Union midfielder, and Julian Hall, who plays for the New York Red Bulls. Both have generated buzz for their potential, but Pochettino said the staff needs to be, in his words, careful with how it handles that hype. It's a signal he wants to manage expectations rather than rush either player into a bigger role than they're ready for.

Meanwhile, the Balogun story has a second track entirely, tied back to the World Cup itself. Belgium's football federation says it still wants answers about how FIFA allowed Balogun to play against them at the tournament, despite what should have been a mandatory one-game suspension. That unresolved frustration has now spilled into FIFA politics. The Belgian federation says it can't back Gianni Infantino, FIFA's president, in his bid for another term, citing both transparency concerns and the way the Balogun situation was handled. It's a reminder that decisions made in the middle of the World Cup are still creating ripple effects for the USMNT program months later.

## the NFL

The Eagles don't kick off until Sunday, but the numbers are already lining up in their favor. Philadelphia opens as a five and a half point home favorite over the Washington Commanders, a bigger spread than the standard three points typically given to a home team. That reflects real confidence in this roster heading into the new season.

Part of that confidence comes from the secondary. New rankings out this week put the Eagles' cornerback group among the top four in the league, built around a trio that could be the best at the position anywhere in football. Cornerback Christian Gonzalez agreed to a four year extension worth 135 million dollars, making him the highest paid cornerback in NFL history. The deal ends a contract dispute and gets him locked up before the Patriots' opener.

Thursday night's season opener in Australia is coming into focus. ## the WNBA

The FIBA World Cup has been the story for basketball fans this week, and Team USA delivered exactly what it needed to close out pool play.

The Americans routed Czechia to finish group stage, bouncing back after a rough outing against Italy earlier in the tournament. It's the kind of statement win that quiets doubts before the knockout rounds begin.

Meanwhile, a different kind of history got made in Belgium's game against Australia. Julie Allemand, a guard for Belgium, broke the World Cup's all-time assist record in the first half of an 80-68 win that clinched her team a quarterfinal spot. She passed Spain's Laia Palau, who had held the mark at 108 career assists. Allemand now sits at 111 for her World Cup career, and the tournament isn't over yet.
