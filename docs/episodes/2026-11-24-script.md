## Tech and AI

The biggest deal in cybersecurity history landed on Monday. CrowdStrike agreed to acquire cloud security platform Zscaler in an all-stock transaction worth roughly 43.9 billion dollars. That tops the 32 billion dollars Google paid for Wiz last year, making this the largest acquisition the cybersecurity industry has ever seen. Under the terms, Zscaler shareholders get 1.135 shares of CrowdStrike stock for every share they hold, a deal valuing Zscaler at about 260 dollars per share, a 34 percent premium. Once it closes, current CrowdStrike shareholders will control about 84 percent of the combined company. The deal still needs sign-off from Zscaler shareholders and regulators, and CrowdStrike expects it to close in the second half of its 2028 fiscal year.

Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia kept generating fallout. Business Insider reported employees at Nvidia's headquarters chanted his name as he arrived back at the office, and Huang told the crowd, "This is my first day at work," apologizing for what he put them through. Semafor reported that President Trump had personally called into Nvidia's board meeting to argue for Huang's reinstatement hours before it happened, with White House communications director Steven Cheung confirming Trump "was totally involved." Nvidia's new board chairman, Harvey Jones, said the board was confident the reinstatement posed no national security risk, despite ongoing government investigations into Huang's conduct and Nvidia's export compliance.

In Texas, outgoing Attorney General Ken Paxton has dropped two lawsuits against out-of-state abortion pill providers, a doctor in California and a nurse practitioner in Delaware, both accused of mailing abortion medication to Texas residents. Paxton filed the withdrawals as he prepares to leave office in January. His successor, Nathan Johnson, has already pledged not to pursue those kinds of cases, and abortion rights advocates, including Nancy Northup of the Center for Reproductive Rights, are calling the withdrawals a win. Shield laws in states like California and Delaware, which protect doctors who mail abortion pills across state lines, had also been complicating Paxton's legal efforts.

Also, research out of Colorado is drawing attention to a widening gap in cervical cancer outcomes for rural women. Nationally, rural women face a cervical cancer rate 25 percent higher than women in cities, and a death rate 42 percent higher. In Colorado, counties like Prowers, Moffat, and Montrose see nearly three times the cervical cancer rate of urban counties in the state. Researchers point to at-home testing kits as one way to close that gap, since rural women often lack easy access to screening, follow-up care, and vaccines.

## the NWSL

Kansas City Current are NWSL champions for the first time. The Current beat Washington two to one on Saturday in front of a sellout crowd in the nation's capital. Spirit President of Soccer Operations Haley Carter thanked González for his contribution but said the club believes a change in leadership is needed. Across his time in charge, González led Washington to 35 wins, 13 draws, and just 12 losses, but could not get the Spirit over the line in three consecutive championship appearances.

Gotham FC is also losing a key piece. Goalkeeper Ann-Katrin Berger is leaving as a free agent for German club FC Union Berlin. Berger joined Gotham in 2024 and helped the team win the inaugural CONCACAF W Champions Cup and the 2025 NWSL Championship. Gotham was eliminated in this year's playoff semifinals, but Berger was still named the NWSL's Goalkeeper of the Year for 2026. She returns to Germany after years playing in France and England, and has made 34 appearances for the German national team.

Meanwhile, the NWSL championship weekend fed straight into the national team picture.
