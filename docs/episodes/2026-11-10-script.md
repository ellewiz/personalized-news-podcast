## Tech and AI

The Jensen Huang story has gone from gossip to a genuine national security review, and the paper trail now stretches back over a year. The New York Times reports that Secretary of State Marco Rubio circulated a memo to officials with high-level security clearances back in May, warning about Huang's relationship with Alice Huang, an executive at the Singapore firm Megaspeed. Around that same time, Jensen Huang was initially left off the list of executives invited to join President Trump's trip to China. Trump personally asked him to join anyway, meeting Air Force One during a layover in Alaska.

Separately, the short-seller Culper Research published a report back in May alleging Nvidia had what it called a China problem, claiming Megaspeed was secretly financed by Alibaba through shell companies, and noting that Jensen Huang and Alice Huang had been photographed together at social events going back to 2024. And Bloomberg reporting from the previous December had already flagged Megaspeed as the largest Southeast Asian buyer of Nvidia chips, quoting Nvidia's own assurances that inspections found no improper chip diversion. Those inspections, it now turns out, were conducted in part by Jensen Huang himself.

Ars Technica reports Nvidia is now examining whether Chinese intelligence targeted Huang through the affair. U.S. officials, according to the Times, suspect Megaspeed has been helping smuggle restricted Nvidia chips into China, though that company has purchased roughly two billion dollars worth of Nvidia products while Nvidia itself booked more than three hundred billion dollars in revenue over the past year.

## the NFL

The biggest trade deadline story in the league belongs to Philadelphia, and it's not a good-news story for Eagles fans hoping for a playoff push. The Eagles shipped defensive tackle and team captain Jordan Davis to the Jacksonville Jaguars on Monday. In return, Howie Roseman, the Eagles' general manager, landed a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-round pick. The Eagles themselves put out a statement saying the Jaguars made an offer Roseman simply could not refuse. That haul now gives Philadelphia ten total draft picks over the next three years, a staggering stockpile for a team that's lost seven straight games. The move has fueled speculation in Philadelphia that the front office isn't just selling off pieces, but could be setting up a bigger shakeup, possibly even a new defensive coordinator down the line. For Jacksonville, this was the third trade the team made to bolster its defense Monday, part of a broader push that also brought in safety Jessie Bates the Third, signaling the Jaguars believe they're built to win now.
