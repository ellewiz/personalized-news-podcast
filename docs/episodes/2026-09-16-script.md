## New Jersey Politics

The Passaic River cleanup is entering a new phase this week. State environmental officials outlined the next steps for remediating the 17-mile stretch of the river that runs from the Dundee Dam down to Newark Bay. That work will now include water and sediment sampling, along with what officials call in-river construction, actual physical work in the riverbed itself. The Passaic has been one of New Jersey's longest-running environmental cleanup fights, and this marks another concrete step toward finishing it.

In Trenton, state lawmakers advanced a bill that would give nursing home residents on Medicaid a small increase in their monthly personal allowance. That allowance covers personal expenses for residents whose care is otherwise paid through Medicaid, and this bill would add a cost-of-living adjustment to it. Advocates for seniors have pushed for this kind of increase for years, arguing the allowance hasn't kept pace with basic costs.

At the federal level, New Jersey Senator Cory Booker clashed with FBI Director Kash Patel during a Senate hearing on election interference. Booker accused Patel of dodging questions about whether the bureau had improperly targeted journalists, at one point calling him a thug. This was a Senate committee hearing, not a state legislative matter, and it centers on the FBI's own conduct rather than anything happening in Trenton.

## Tech and AI

Meta appears to be preparing a new version of its smart glasses that ditches the camera entirely. The Information reports the device, codenamed Luna, could debut within weeks. The timing isn't a coincidence. Meta's existing glasses have drawn sustained criticism over their built-in recording capability, and a camera-free model would sidestep that entire controversy.

That controversy fits into a bigger pattern showing up in new polling. The New York Times and Siena University surveyed over fifteen hundred likely voters and found that 61 percent oppose building new data centers to power AI systems. Politicians are already responding to that sentiment, according to the report, which helps explain why AI companies are leaning harder on Washington directly.

Two of the biggest players in that space, Anthropic and OpenAI, are pushing federal officials to treat them as too significant to fail, essentially seeking government backing that would cement their position at the top of the industry. Meanwhile, an unlikely political pairing is pushing in a different direction. Bernie Sanders and Steve Bannon, the former Trump strategist, are both appearing at a Washington event this week calling for stronger AI safety guardrails, even as the Trump administration argues that AI safety concerns are overblown and could let China get ahead.

On the infrastructure side, the power problem behind all this AI buildout is getting harder to ignore. A new estimate says the United States will need 110 billion dollars in new electricity generation by 2030 just to keep pace with data center demand. The Department of Energy is also backing higher-enriched uranium production aimed at powering future data centers, though output remains slow for now. And with Nvidia's networking hardware acting as a bottleneck for scaling AI systems, two challengers, the Intel spinoff Cornelis and a newcomer called Delos Data, are pitching open alternatives to Nvidia's NVLink connections between chips.

On the security front, Apple pushed out a record number of patches this week, addressing a large batch of vulnerabilities across its operating systems. Separately, Apple's security research team published a detailed paper on what it calls Apple Reference Image, a system designed to verify photos are authentic without exposing the photographer's identity or the image itself, even to Apple. The company says the images are validated and signed through its private cloud compute system, which it says keeps the actual pixel data inaccessible to anyone, including Apple itself.

Elsewhere in security, Cisco is warning that a critical flaw in its email security appliances can be triggered by nothing more than a malicious email, and that attackers are already exploiting it in the wild. The Texas utility CenterPoint Energy confirmed a breach after a forum post claimed nearly seven and a half million files were stolen. And BT customers in the UK reported receiving hundreds of unwanted password reset codes in rapid bursts, though the telecom giant says accounts remain secure.

Microsoft has set October 7th for a Windows and Surface event in San Francisco, its first major Windows showcase in more than two years, with a focus on how local AI processing will shape the platform going forward.

And SpaceX says its Starship rocket is ready for an orbital attempt next week, a milestone the company has been chasing through a long stretch of test flights.
