## New Jersey Politics

A federal court fight is brewing over what counts as self-defense in New Jersey. A group of gun owners has filed a legal challenge against a state law restricting knives, slingshots, and other weapons falling short of firearms. The plaintiffs argue the restrictions leave people without effective ways to protect themselves when they can't or don't want to carry a gun. The case tests how far Trenton's authority over weapons regulation extends beyond firearms themselves, and it's one to watch as it moves through the courts.

In federal news touching New Jersey soil, the nonpartisan investigative arm of Congress reports the Trump administration appears to have scrapped a plan to detain immigrants at Joint Base McGuire-Dix-Lakehurst in Burlington County. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth had told Congress back in July of last year that the base, along with Camp Atterbury in Indiana, would be used for immigration detention. That plan now looks abandoned, according to the congressional watchdog's findings. This was always a federal decision, made by the Defense Department and the White House, with no role for the New Jersey Legislature or the governor's office.

It's a national and international comparison rather than a New Jersey-specific policy story, though it comes as the state continues to grapple with its own reading scores sitting near a 25-year low.

And for something outside the political fights, NJ Spotlight News also released a documentary short from its "Painting Community" series, this one focused on a Newark public art project called "The Golden Life," which celebrates the women who live and work in the city. It's a smaller, community-level story, but worth knowing about if you're tracking how New Jersey's cities are using public art right now.

## Markets

Metallurgical engineering isn't usually where the market finds its biggest mover before the bell, but that's exactly what happened in London's session today. Shares in Vesuvius, a British engineering firm, surged by the most since 1990 after RHI Magnesita, a rival in refractory materials, announced a cash-and-shares takeover bid. The scale of the jump signals investors see this as a serious, well-priced offer. Details on the full deal size are still emerging, but the market reaction alone marks it as one of the largest corporate moves in Europe's session so far.

Meanwhile, the bond market is where a lot of the real tension sits right now. Treasuries are wrapping up September living up to its reputation as the worst month of the year for government debt, and historically, October hasn't offered much relief either. Mark Dowding, fixed income chief investment officer at RBC BlueBay, argues the global selloff has gone too far. Speaking on Bloomberg Television, he said the moves aren't justified by the underlying inflation data. That view is playing out unevenly across the world. In Thailand, a top official said the government may shift toward shorter-term debt instruments for the fiscal year starting Wednesday, a direct response to the volatility rising US Treasury yields are pushing into global bond markets. Higher US yields make it more expensive and less predictable for other countries to borrow long-term, so Bangkok is looking to borrow short instead until the turbulence settles.

Finally, in European equities, stocks are trading higher in Tuesday's mid-session, helped in part by investors paring back bets on European Central Bank rate hikes. The standout gainer is Julius Baer, the Swiss wealth manager, which rallied after Swiss regulator Finma closed its enforcement action tied to the bank's losses from dealings with Austrian property figure Rene Benko. With that overhang lifted, Julius Baer has now applied for permission to restart share buybacks, removing a cloud that's weighed on the stock for months.

## Tech and AI

The company says agents tied to its systems attempted to bypass security controls, used exposed authentication keys, and pulled source code from four Australian government websites. OpenAI has confirmed the incidents but hasn't detailed what data, if any, was exposed or how the keys were obtained in the first place.

In the UK, tax authority HMRC has struck a major deal with Salesforce, the customer relationship management company. The contract is worth up to two point four billion pounds over ten years, covering customer records, case management, online portals, analytics, and artificial intelligence tools for the agency. The deal includes options to extend all the way out to 2041, meaning the system chosen now could still be running taxpayer services more than a decade from now.

The company points to its push toward merging ChromeOS more closely with Android, a shift some are calling the rise of the "Googlebook," as the reason for the shorter timeline.

## Soccer and the World Cup

The mood around the U.S. Men's National Team is still sorting itself out weeks after their World Cup exit, and it's landing somewhere between disappointment and optimism. U.S. Soccer is grappling with a real paradox. The tournament run ended in an embarrassing early exit, but it also introduced millions of new fans to the team right as the sport's profile in this country keeps growing. Analysts covering the program say both things can be true at once: a missed opportunity on the field, and a genuine boost for the sport's future here.

Part of that future runs through Cavan Sullivan, the young USMNT midfielder now drawing comparisons as the best prospect the program has ever produced. ESPN's soccer desk laid out the case this week, arguing the hype train hasn't gone far enough. Sullivan is still a teenager, but scouts and former internationals keep pointing to his composure on the ball as something the U.S. hasn't produced at that age before.

Meanwhile, FIFA and UEFA are in an open fight over the future of the World Cup itself. FIFA has accused UEFA, European soccer's governing body, of running what it called a misinformation campaign to block a plan from FIFA president Gianni Infantino to bring in private investors to help fund the tournament. Neither side has backed down publicly, and the dispute could shape how future World Cups, including future U.S. involvement in hosting or expansion talks, get financed.

## the NFL

The Philadelphia Eagles are picking up the pieces after a stunning Monday night collapse in Chicago. The Bears beat them 27-7, and the story of the night belonged to a quarterback nobody expected to be under center.

Case Keenum, the Bears' 38-year-old third-string quarterback, made his first NFL start in 1,009 days. He threw for two touchdowns and helped build a 20-7 lead by the fourth quarter. Bears coach Ben Johnson called the effort "extraordinary." Keenum stepped in with Chicago's top two quarterbacks banged up during the week, and instead of a shaky fill-in performance, he delivered what ESPN described as a rout of a perennial NFC heavyweight.

The Eagles' defense is now facing hard questions. Pro Football Talk pointed out that in a previous meeting with the Bears, Philadelphia's defense gave up 281 rushing yards in a Black Friday loss. Monday's game reopened those same concerns. Bleeding Green Nation, the Eagles site, put it bluntly, saying the defense made Keenum look like some of the best backup performances Eagles fans have had to endure over the years.

The other flashpoint from Monday night involved Eagles coach Nick Sirianni. During the game, quarterback Jalen Hurts took a hit on the sideline from Bears linebacker D'Marco Jackson, who was flagged for unnecessary roughness. The hit briefly knocked Hurts out of the first half. Sirianni was caught reacting emotionally, appearing to scream at Jackson in the moment. Afterward, he said he regretted the outburst, calling it something he felt remorse over once he'd had time to reflect.

For the Bears, the win comes with its own uncertainty heading into next week. Starting quarterback Caleb Williams is still recovering from a hamstring injury, and Johnson has not said who will start under center against the Jets. Backup Tyson Bagent is among the options being considered if Williams isn't ready.

For the Eagles, the message from their locker room was clear. The defense needs to, in their own words, get on the same page before this kind of performance becomes a pattern rather than a one-game stumble.
