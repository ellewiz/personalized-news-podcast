## New Jersey Politics

New Jersey has joined a multistate lawsuit challenging the Trump administration's move to pay private wind developers to cancel their offshore lease agreements. The state attorney general's office signed onto the suit this week, arguing the payouts undercut projects New Jersey has been counting on for its clean energy targets. The case now heads into federal court, where New Jersey will argue alongside other states that the federal government overstepped its authority in structuring those cancellation payments.

Meanwhile, the fight over New Jersey's voter rolls is heating up again. The Republican National Committee has filed a new lawsuit against New Jersey election officials, demanding they turn over records tied to last year's noncitizen voting error. This is the second time Republicans have sued over the same records dispute, and it keeps pressure on the state's election administration heading into the fall campaign season.

That backdrop of election tension is playing out alongside anxiety among New Jersey's own congressional Democrats over federal spending. Congressman Josh Gottheimer says the national debt crossing forty trillion dollars is squeezing what Washington can still offer struggling families, telling NJ Spotlight News there are, in his words, fewer and fewer nickels to help people. With midterm elections weeks away, he and other New Jersey Democrats say inflation and federal budget fights are colliding right as voters are paying closest attention.

Finally, a Rutgers University story worth noting for anyone who remembers the Tyler Clementi case. A scholar is now pushing back on the narrative that Rutgers was a hostile environment for LGBTQ students at the time of Clementi's death sixteen years ago. The author argues the university actually provided strong support services for LGBTQ students both before and after the tragedy, and is hoping to correct what she sees as a lasting misconception about the school's record.

## the NFL
