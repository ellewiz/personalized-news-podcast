## Markets

Wall Street just closed out one of its roughest stretches in years, and futures are pointing to a bounce before the opening bell. The S&P 500 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average both fell into correction territory last week, with the broader selloff wiping out more than five trillion dollars in S&P 500 value. The damage traces back to a violent rotation out of technology shares, as investors grew nervous about how much of the market's gains have been riding on a small handful of AI-linked giants. In premarket trading, futures are jumping as dip buyers move in, and Morgan Stanley strategists, including Mike Wilson, are calling this a buying opportunity too good to pass up. Not every desk agrees that the worst is over, but the early tone heading into the US session is one of stabilization rather than panic. That shift is also showing up in the bond market, where Treasuries are edging lower as investors unwind some of the haven buying they piled into during the worst of the selloff.

The pain from that selloff wasn't confined to the United States. In Taiwan, where markets have already closed for the day, stockbroker Julia Lee says margin calls soared roughly two-hundred-fold last week as traders who'd piled into AI-linked bets got forced out of their positions. She told Bloomberg she started sending personal messages to clients to help them manage the stress of riding out the losses. It's a sign of how global the exposure to the AI trade had become, stretching from Wall Street's biggest tech names all the way to leveraged retail traders across Asia.

Meanwhile, European natural gas prices are climbing in Monday's session, now underway across London, Frankfurt and Paris. ## Tech and AI

Security researchers at Google's Mandiant unit say a critical bug in VMware's vCenter software was being exploited months before VMware ever disclosed it. VMware revealed the flaw, tracked as CVE-2026-123890, last Thursday, warning it allows unauthorized access to the console that manages host and guest virtual machines. Mandiant says a China-linked group it calls Armory Castle knew about the bug back in July and was actively exploiting it by September. The researchers say the group breaks in through compromised remote access appliances from BeyondTrust, then uses a custom tool called BONESAW to forge authentication tokens, bypass multi-factor authentication, and impersonate any user on the network once it finds vCenter running.

The technology outages that have piled up this month show no signs of stopping. Over roughly the past week, a nationwide FAA ground stop, a multi-day Outlook outage that froze corporate email, and a banking outage at JPMorgan Chase have all made headlines. Delta Air Lines also reported significant delays for flights in and out of its Atlanta hub after a system that calculates aircraft weight and balance went down. Jay Clayton, the Director of National Intelligence, told CNN that Americans should expect continued investment in upgrading systems and cybersecurity, saying bumps are inevitable whenever there's a major shift in computing and applications. Microsoft President Brad Smith has also weighed in on the trend, which analysts expect to continue as aging infrastructure gets pushed harder by new software.

In space news, Europe's BepiColombo spacecraft has reached Mercury, becoming only the third mission ever to arrive there, but not without trouble. The mission, a joint effort between the European Space Agency and Japan's space agency JAXA, had to be rescheduled after its thrusters underperformed back in 2024, delaying arrival from mid-2025 to this past weekend. Ground controllers sent commands to insert the craft into orbit on Friday, and it phoned home on Sunday confirming it had made it. But the European Space Agency says the orbital insertion burn came up short because of a software issue. The agency says the spacecraft itself is safe, though the exact state of its orbit is still being worked out.

NASA, meanwhile, is dealing with its own glitches. The Perseverance rover on Mars has paused science operations because of a communications problem causing low data rates. More seriously, the aging Odyssey orbiter dropped into safe mode earlier this month after one of its remaining reaction wheels stopped spinning. Odyssey has been orbiting Mars since 2001, decades past its original two-and-a-half-year mission, and has already lost one of its four reaction wheels back in 2013. Engineers say the wheel did respond when commanded, which is encouraging, but the orbiter needs at least three working wheels to orient itself properly, or it has to fall back on thruster fuel that's in limited supply.

In India, the Bombay High Court has sided with Meta in a dispute with an advertising agency called Blink Digital, refusing to order Meta to restore four disabled Business Manager accounts that together ran about nine thousand advertising accounts. Justice Sandeep Marne ruled that Blink Digital has no inherent right to use Meta's platforms for its business, and that Meta is not a government agency bound by public duty. The court pointed to material showing Blink Digital had marketed what it called "ban-proof" advertisements with no spending limits to its clients. The judge didn't rule on whether Meta's response was proportionate, and left the door open for Blink Digital to seek damages later if it can prove a breach of contract at trial, or to ask Meta to reinstate the accounts if it agrees to follow Meta's policies going forward.

Separately in India, the Delhi High Court has ruled it has no jurisdiction to order search engines to de-index a court order issued by a different High Court, even when that order is accessible to people in Delhi. The case involved a doctor trying to get a 2019 Gauhati High Court bail order removed from Google and Indian Kanoon search results. Justice Swarana Kanta Sharma said allowing jurisdiction based on accessibility alone would let every High Court in the country claim authority over any other court's records, inviting what the judge called forum shopping. The doctor has been left free to take the request to the Gauhati High Court instead.

And Uttar Pradesh's state government has put out a tender seeking a contractor to build a single artificial intelligence platform shared across every state department, linked to a twelve-digit family identifier already used for welfare programs. The request for proposals, published by the state's e-governance center, lists twelve initial uses for the system, including analyzing police records, monitoring social media, reviewing property transactions, and drafting responses to right-to-information requests. Whoever wins the contract will get twelve months to build the platform, followed by two years of running and maintaining it.

## the NWSL

The Kansas City Current are NWSL champions for the first time in club history. They beat the Washington Spirit 2-1 at Audi Field in Washington on Saturday, in front of more than 19,000 fans.

Vanessa DiBernardo opened the scoring with an Olimpico, a goal curled directly in from a corner kick. Substitute Debinha scored the eventual game-winner. It was a physical, foul-heavy final from the opening whistle. Washington's Trinity Rodman went down holding her shoulder in just the fifth minute after a hard challenge from Kansas City rookie defender Laney Rouse. The first half alone produced 19 fouls, a championship record. Kansas City head coach Chris Armas credited that early physicality for setting the tone, with defender Izzy Rodriguez saying the team wanted to put Washington in an uncomfortable position right from kickoff.

For Washington, it's a third straight NWSL Championship defeat, and their first-ever loss in a home playoff game at Audi Field, a stadium that had built a reputation for an electric, playoff-tested crowd. Rodman called the loss the toughest of the three, saying it was still too fresh to put into words, though she pointed to a season still worth celebrating. Washington's roster for this final leaned heavily international, with eight players from outside the U.S. in the starting lineup, the most international lineup in championship history. That's a sharp contrast to the club's only other title, back in 2021, when all eleven starters were American.

Kansas City midfielder Croix Bethune, the league's two-time Midfielder of the Year, set the NWSL's single-season assists record this year after transferring from Washington. She wasn't named an MVP finalist, but her performances were a significant factor in Kansas City reaching, and now winning, its first championship.
