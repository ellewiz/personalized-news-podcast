## New Jersey Politics

A state appeals panel handed Rutgers University a win this week, ruling that the school does not need New Jersey's approval to launch new degree programs. Two judges rejected the state's argument that it holds veto power over Rutgers' academic offerings, a decision that strengthens the university's independence from Trenton on curriculum decisions.

Meanwhile, Trenton is facing pressure from Washington. House Republicans have subpoenaed Governor Sherrill's administration, demanding records on how roughly six thousand people who identified themselves as noncitizens ended up registered to vote in New Jersey. The subpoena puts the state's own voter rolls under federal scrutiny, and it's now up to the administration to decide how much it hands over.

New Jersey's insurance regulators also made news this week. The state fined Geico and its affiliated companies after investigators found the company overbilled New Jersey drivers by ten and a half million dollars on car insurance policies in 2023 and 2024. Geico has to repay that money to the motorists who were overcharged.

There's also a warning sign for the state's finances. A new commentary piece out this week says the state-run health benefits plan covering public workers is nearing collapse, and argues that if lawmakers don't act, the fallout could raise health costs for residents well beyond the public workforce.

On the court system, a special master's report detailed in New Jersey Spotlight News finds that the state's courts are failing disabled children and their families, pointing to persistent delays and what the report describes as judicial bias that slows down due process for these families.

Local health officials are also sounding an alarm about vapes. An op-ed from a New Jersey local health officer says smoke shops and unregulated vape sales have overwhelmed local inspectors, who don't have the staff or authority to keep up with the volume of illegal product moving through New Jersey towns.

Over in Camden County, Cherry Hill's school district is holding community meetings to grapple with what it's calling an explosion of artificial intelligence use among students, asking parents and residents for input on how the schools should respond.

And a new study covering New Jersey alongside several other states finds that even patients with commercial health insurance are struggling to get appointments for mental healthcare, a gap researchers say persists despite coverage requirements on paper.

## Tech and AI

Tesla's third quarter earnings landed today with a mix of big promises and shrinking numbers. The company posted net income of 1.7 billion dollars on revenue of 28 billion dollars, with profit falling 43 percent compared to a year earlier. Revenue was essentially flat, even after Tesla's recent Roadster demo generated plenty of buzz. The company's energy storage business also shrank during the quarter. On the earnings call, Tesla's Ashok Elluswamy, the company's vice president for autonomous driving, said owners will finally be able to add their own cars to Tesla's robotaxi fleet starting next year. Elon Musk has promised that capability for years, so the new deadline gives Tesla something concrete to be judged against.

Musk also made news on two other fronts today. The Wall Street Journal reported that he personally ordered a slowdown of Starlink service in Ukraine, then blamed the move on President Trump. Both Musk and Trump denied giving that order. Separately, Musk said merging SpaceX and Tesla would be, in his words, a good idea, telling reporters there's a lot of merit to combining the two companies.

Meta also reported third quarter earnings on Wednesday. The company said it's creating a new class of non-voting shares to help fund its AI push, with trading set to begin in 2027; the money raised will also go toward acquisitions and employee pay. Meta also disclosed that a child safety settlement cost it dearly this quarter. The company paid nearly 9.9 billion dollars toward a settlement it reached with U.S. attorneys general in August, worth 17.1 billion dollars in total. Meta says that without the payout, its income from operations would have topped 22.8 billion dollars for the quarter.

Meanwhile, a security mess tied to the PHP programming language knocked major parts of the internet offline on Wednesday. PHP underpins a huge share of the web, including most WordPress sites, and two newly discovered vulnerabilities disrupted 4chan, DeviantArt, and Wikipedia, among many others. Developers rushed out a critical patch and urged unpatched site owners to take their servers offline as a precaution, since exploits were already circulating publicly. More emergency patches are reportedly on the way.
