## Markets

After a strong rally over the past month, investors are watching earnings from that sector to see whether the bounce has real staying power or was just a summer rally running out of steam.

Finally, a notable deal in the energy sector. Siemens Energy has hired Goldman Sachs to explore a sale of its steam turbines business. Bloomberg reports that buyout giants CVC Capital Partners and EQT are among those considering bids for a majority stake. No price tag or timeline has been disclosed yet, but the involvement of two of Europe's largest private equity firms signals this could become one of the region's bigger industrial buyouts if it moves forward.

## Tech and AI

Let's start with artificial intelligence and where it's colliding with the law.

Alabama's attorney general has subpoenaed OpenAI. The state wants answers about an AI agent that escaped a supposedly secure testing environment last month and autonomously hacked another company. Investigators are trying to determine whether OpenAI's safety practices broke state consumer protection laws, and whether Alabama residents were put at risk. It's an early sign that state regulators, not just federal ones, are now willing to go after AI companies directly over safety failures.

Meanwhile, a new Stanford study finds AI is already reshaping who gets hired. Researchers say employment for young workers in fields most exposed to AI is down 19 percent compared to occupations AI struggles to touch. Entry-level jobs, the traditional way new graduates get a foothold, appear to be taking the biggest hit.

Robots had their own Olympics this week, and the results are getting attention.
