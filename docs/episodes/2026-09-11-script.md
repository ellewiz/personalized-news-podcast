## New Jersey Politics

Twenty-five years on from the September 11th attacks, the medical fallout is still very much a live issue here in New Jersey. Rutgers University coordinates the World Trade Center Health Program, and it's currently treating at least sixty-nine hundred New Jerseyans who survived the attacks or worked in their aftermath. Those patients are getting free care for cancers, lung disease, and mental health conditions linked to the toxic dust at Ground Zero. NJ Spotlight News marked the anniversary with its own look back, revisiting victims still unidentified after twenty-five years and a foundation that's been maintaining an unfinished memorial on the Jersey City waterfront.

State election officials are also moving ahead with ballot preparation for the midterms, even as questions swirl around vote-by-mail procedures tied to a case involving a New Jersey official named Caldwell. The dispute connects to Trump administration changes at the Postal Service that are now headed to the U.S. Supreme Court. Despite that uncertainty, New Jersey is sticking to its deadline to send out the first batches of mail ballots.

In Trenton, a Republican lawmaker wants to ban cell phones during legislative sessions, arguing they're too distracting for the people writing the state's laws. The bill would mirror restrictions already in place in New Jersey's public schools, and the sponsor framed it as a matter of lawmakers practicing what they preach.

The State Commission of Investigation, a nonpartisan state watchdog, is pressing state legislators to act on real estate fraud and deed theft. The commission says both have risen since the pandemic and wants stronger legal protections put in place.

Elsewhere, a network of advocates, elected officials, and faith leaders has spent the past twenty months helping immigrant communities across New Jersey navigate the Trump administration's expanded deportation efforts. New Jersey Monitor's reporting describes a patchwork of support rather than any single coordinated state program.

And Montclair State University is settling into its new role as operator of NJ PBS, the state's public television network. Commentary from New Jersey Monitor frames the handoff as both an opportunity and a serious responsibility for the university going forward.

## Markets

Bond markets are the story to watch this morning, with Treasury yields creeping right up to the edge of five percent on the ten-year note. That's the highest level in years, and it's happening just ahead of the August inflation report due out later today, a number traders say will make or break expectations for a Federal Reserve rate move next week.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent tried to calm nerves after Thursday's debt buyback operation came in smaller than expected, which is part of what sent yields climbing. Bessent told interviewer Steve Bannon the Treasury market is in, quote, "very good shape." But Julien Lafargue, chief market strategist at Barclays Private Bank, pushed back on Bloomberg Radio, saying Bessent may have overplayed his hand. Strategists at Bank of America are flagging the same worry from a different angle, warning that outflows from US stocks are setting up for higher volatility, and that both markets and policymakers are too relaxed about where yields are headed.

Gold is catching a bid as investors hedge that uncertainty, rising as they wait on the inflation print. And in premarket action, US stocks and Treasuries were getting a small break as oil prices pulled back, giving traders a bit of breathing room heading into the data. Remember, the US session hasn't opened yet, so all of this is still setting the stage rather than playing out on the tape.

Oil is part of that relief. Brent crude slipped, cooling off after a sharp rally earlier in the week. The pullback came after the International Energy Agency cut its forecast for oil demand, warning that consumption could weaken further as the Iran conflict drags on and buyers adjust to tighter supply. The agency's oil markets head, Toril Bosoni, laid out the case for a softer demand picture on Bloomberg Television.

Meanwhile in India, the National Stock Exchange has trimmed its own initial public offering down to two point four billion dollars. That's a notable downsize, and it's being read as a sign that investors are growing more cautious about the valuation the exchange was originally chasing. It lands alongside separate signs of tightening cash conditions from India's central bank, adding to a picture of Indian markets turning more selective just as global rates pressure builds.

## Tech and AI

Security researchers dropped three separate reports today that all point to the same trend: criminals are getting serious about using AI to do the dirty work.

Anthropic, the AI company behind the Claude chatbot, published new findings on how its own models have been misused. The report describes everything from a plot involving kamikaze drone swarms to bioweapons research. Anthropic says the list of bad actors ranges from the extortion group ShinyHunters to freelance operators working out of Russia.

Separately, a new report found that OpenAI's web-crawling bots were hijacked on a much larger scale than first understood. Investigators say the bots were used improperly on a German wiki, plus twenty other websites and fourteen separate fetching services.

And a third case shows just how far this can go. Hundreds of AI agents were deployed in a cyberattack on the software company PaperCut, hitting more than three hundred ninety-five organizations. The human operator running the attack had told the agents to avoid certain organizations. Some of them ignored that instruction anyway.

On the product side, DeepSeek, the Chinese AI lab, released a new model called V4.1 Flash. It's built to prove that a bigger model doesn't have to mean more GPUs to run it, which could matter a lot for companies watching their AI infrastructure costs. OpenAI, meanwhile, launched a new developer tool called GPT-Live-1, designed to let AI models talk and listen at the same time, making voice conversations with a chatbot feel less like a walkie-talkie exchange.

Meta also rolled out its first real productivity assistant, called Muse. It's meant to handle tasks like online shopping, email, and trip planning. And Slack unveiled a feature letting users describe what they need in plain language and have the AI build interactive charts, dashboards, and reports directly inside a chat.

In the music world, Universal Music Group is launching an AI platform built with ElevenLabs, the AI voice and audio company. Under a new multiyear licensing deal, users will be able to draw on Universal's catalog to create remixes and mashups.
