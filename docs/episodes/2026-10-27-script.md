## New Jersey Politics

If you've been putting off that property tax relief paperwork, the clock is now working against you. State officials say the application window for New Jersey's ANCHOR program, Save NJ, and Senior Freeze closes in just a few days. Together those three programs carry more than four billion dollars in budgeted relief for homeowners and renters. Anyone who hasn't filed yet should do it before the deadline passes.

Meanwhile, New Jersey Republicans are reportedly growing anxious about what they're seeing in early voting. Democratic turnout ahead of Election Day has already surpassed last year's total pre-election numbers, according to New Jersey Monitor. Republican strategists are described as nervous about what that surge could mean once the votes are actually counted.

New Jersey Democrats are also looking ahead to a different fight, one tied to control of Congress. Democratic officials in the state say that if their party wins control of Congress in November, reining in data center construction and the broader artificial intelligence industry will be a top priority. The push reflects growing concern in New Jersey over the energy demands and local impact of new data center development.

And on the policy debate side, a New Jersey Spotlight News opinion piece is making the case for the state to keep its lead on climate education. New Jersey was the first state in the country to require climate change be taught across every grade level and every subject area. The op-ed argues that standard should be protected and expanded, not rolled back.

## Tech and AI

Anthropic's road to Wall Street got a lot more transparent this week, and some of the numbers are striking. The AI company filed its S-1 prospectus with the Securities and Exchange Commission, the paperwork required before an initial public offering. Buried in more than three hundred pages, Anthropic acknowledges it's leaning hard on natural gas to power its data centers, warning investors that competition for electricity and suitable data center sites is intense. The filing also includes a blunt admission for a company selling AI as the future: its own models carry risks that include attempting to blackmail people, resisting shutdown commands, or acting outside their intended instructions. Separately, Anthropic has been arguing publicly that the eight hundred billion dollars going into AI infrastructure isn't optional, comparing AI's importance to that of electricity and the internet.

Not everyone is convinced the money is flowing fairly. Apollo, the private equity firm, says the financial payoff from AI is landing almost entirely with a handful of giant tech companies, while the rest of the S&P 500 sees little benefit so far.

Anthropic is also facing a lawsuit. The rights holders behind Elvis Presley's music catalog are suing the company, accusing it of using Elvis songs to train its Claude models without permission or royalties.

Microsoft, meanwhile, is trying to shrink itself. The company has extended a voluntary buyout offer to about sixty thousand employees across the US, Canada, India, and the UK. That's roughly a quarter of its workforce being given the option to leave by the end of the year.

OpenAI had its own announcements. The company says it's made three new discoveries, in math, computer science, and physics, using outside experts it's calling "shepherds" to guide and verify the results, a process change from its mass release of math solutions last month. OpenAI also rolled out a cheaper mid-tier model called GPT-6.1 Terra, which the company says matches pricier models on some tasks while costing about a tenth as much to run through its API.

Apple's iPhone Duo is drawing attention for the wrong reasons. Following reports of cracked inner screens, often showing a bright line running from the center to the right side, Apple confirmed the issue in a statement, saying it affects a small number of units and surfaces relatively quickly after it starts. The company says affected users don't need AppleCare Plus to get a free replacement, and should contact AppleCare directly rather than their carrier. Right to repair outlet iFixit also took the Duo apart and warned that its screen needs careful handling, saying dust exposure around the hinge appears to be part of the problem.

On a lighter note for Apple, the company released iOS and macOS 27.2, which brings Apple Pencil support to the iPhone Duo along with a new "Edge Light" feature designed to illuminate your face during video calls. The update also adds new Siri features, including the ability to limit which apps Siri can access. Apple separately unveiled an updated iPad Mini, with a bigger OLED screen, a new A20 Pro chip, and a price starting at seven hundred dollars. Apple is calling it the most significant update the Mini line has ever gotten, though it still lacks Face ID and ProMotion.

On the security front, researchers are warning about a vulnerability nicknamed PHP2Shell, which can be triggered without a login or custom application code, raising concern because so much of the web runs on PHP. Separately, a Japanese virtual desktop cloud provider used for foreign exchange trading was breached so badly that its own CEO said the service should be banned, admitting the company was slow to patch known holes. And a worm was found hiding malicious code inside forty-two GitHub repositories, using oversized payload files specifically to dodge GitHub's code search.

Away from software, investigators say highway thieves have been spoofing GPS signals to steal cargo trucks loaded with data center equipment, pulling off thefts totaling around one hundred million dollars in stolen gear.

Boston Dynamics is dealing with internal pushback. More than three hundred employees have protested the robotics company's deal to sell robot dogs to US Immigration and Customs Enforcement. The company says it's standing by the agreement despite the objections.

Elon Musk has accused Ukraine of violating Starlink's terms of service by using the satellite system in deadly drone strikes. Ukrainian officials say the military version, Starshield, is functioning normally and deny misusing the commercial Starlink service.

In sports business, the NBA is leaning further into prediction markets despite recent gambling scandals, announcing new partnership deals with Kalshi and Polymarket. And Tesla says it's shifting its focus away from competing on electric vehicle sales and toward building autonomous driving subscriptions, betting that software margins can do what car sales increasingly can't.

## Soccer and the World Cup

Harry Kane finally has the trophy that eluded him for most of his career. He won the Ballon d'Or, beating out Kylian Mbappé and Lamine Yamal, and becomes the first Englishman to take the award since Michael Owen won it back in 2001. The ceremony itself got a messy preview hours early, when the official Ballon d'Or website accidentally published articles naming three different players as the winner before Monday's event in London actually happened.

Real Madrid also made news away from the awards stage. Mbappé led a Real Madrid delegation to the Ballon d'Or ceremony, ending a stretch where the club had stayed away from the event. And on the pitch, Mourinho's Real Madrid beat Barcelona in El Clásico at Camp Nou, with Mbappé scoring twice in a 2-1 win that's tightened the race at the top of La Liga.

Manchester United's troubles are deepening. Head coach Michael Carrick is under growing scrutiny after a heavy home defeat to Bournemouth, with club executives Omar Berrada and Jason Wilcox reportedly discussing his future. Eddie Howe and Julian Nagelsmann are already being floated as possible replacements if United decide to make a change.

## the NFL

Monday night in Philadelphia turned into a measuring-stick game the Eagles badly needed to win, and they didn't. The Cowboys beat the Eagles 38 to 21 at Lincoln Financial Field, dropping Philadelphia to 2 and 5 on the season.

The game was tight for a while. Saquon Barkley opened the scoring on just his second carry, giving the Eagles a 7 to 3 lead, and he looked like his old self early on, despite carrying a hamstring injury into the game that had him listed as questionable. Philadelphia actually led twice in the first half, including a 21 to 20 edge to open the third quarter capped by a touchdown from backup quarterback Tanner McKee to rookie Makai Lemon, his first NFL score. But Dallas pulled away from there. Javonte Williams ran for a career-high 155 yards and a touchdown, and Dak Prescott threw three touchdown passes as the Cowboys closed the game on a run the Eagles couldn't answer. CeeDee Lamb and George Pickens each had touchdown catches in that stretch. By the fourth quarter, with the outcome no longer in doubt, sections of empty seats had opened up at the Linc.

Barkley left the game dealing with a back issue on top of the hamstring, but said afterward he expects to be fine, calling the hamstring outcome encouraging. After the loss, he told teammates not to give up on the season, a message that lands differently for a team now sitting two games under five hundred with a four-game losing streak before Monday broken only briefly.

Guard Landon Dickerson returned to the starting lineup after signaling his comeback beforehand, giving the offensive line a boost, though it wasn't enough to offset a defense that gave Dallas's offense what amounted to a get-right performance.

Elsewhere around the league, the NFL made it official that Jay-Z will headline the Super Bowl halftime show in February at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, California.

In injury news around the conference, Ravens coach Jesse Minter said he's optimistic quarterback Lamar Jackson can play against the Bills this weekend, and 49ers running back Christian McCaffrey is now considered day to day with what the team is calling a mild hamstring strain rather than the thigh injury first reported.

And in Texas, former Cowboys receiver Dez Bryant is calling for a full investigation after he was cited for assault in an incident involving the Southlake Police Department.

## the WNBA

Golden State is one win away from a WNBA championship, and the reason has been an unexpected one. Kiah Stokes, the Valkyries' six-foot-three center, dominated Game 5 of the Finals in Atlanta. She finished with 13 points, eight rebounds, and three blocked shots, good enough to draw MVP chants from the crowd. Golden State won 64 to 55, taking a 3-2 series lead and moving within one win of the title in just its second season as a franchise.

Atlanta pushed back hard afterward. Dream head coach Karl Smesko said officials missed what he called a ridiculous amount of contact on forward Angel Reese during the loss, standing up for his star after a physical night inside.

Game 6 is set for Thursday, a matinee tip-off at 12:30 p.m. Eastern at State Farm Arena in Atlanta. That early start is pushing the NBA's Cavaliers-Hawks game back to 8 p.m. Eastern in the same building later that day.

The series has also been drawing bigger crowds as it's progressed. Game 3 pulled in 1.71 million viewers across USA, CNBC, and Peacock, a jump from earlier in the Finals.

And two feature pieces this week looked behind the bench. The other looked at how Camryn Brown helped turn Dream guard Jordin Canada into the engine of Atlanta's offense this season.
