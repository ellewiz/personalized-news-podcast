## Markets

Brent crude dropped after a report that President Trump wants to give diplomacy with Iran more time before resuming strikes that had threatened energy flows out of the region. That slide in oil prices is doing a lot of work for sentiment heading into the US open. US equity futures are higher, as falling oil helps stocks claw back from a rough stretch in the artificial intelligence trade. Copper also bounced off its lowest close in a week, as the tech selloff eased and cheaper oil calmed broader growth fears. And gold, which hit a one-year low Friday, is climbing back as dip buyers step in and lower oil prices ease some of the inflation worry that had been weighing on the metal. Zoom out further and there's a bigger debate brewing about oil's long-term direction. Jeff Currie, the CEO and founder of Real Macro, told Bloomberg at the Adipec conference in Abu Dhabi that physical oil markets are pricing in a permanent shift in energy strategy worldwide, with long-dated Brent contracts near ninety-nine dollars a barrel. He argues the real driver isn't short-term conflict at all, but a global push toward energy security and redundant supply.

## Tech and AI

North Korea's fingerprints are now officially on one of the biggest crypto heists in history. The FBI says the regime was behind the six-point-three-billion-dollar theft that hit Binance, the cryptocurrency exchange, on October 29th. The agency is calling the hacking campaign "TraderTraitor" and says it's observed the same actors converting stolen tokens into Ethereum to launder the funds. The FBI didn't share many other details about how the investigation is proceeding.

Meanwhile in enterprise tech, Amazon has sent what The Register describes as a mixed message to its own workforce about artificial intelligence. The memo has left a lot of software engineers at the company uneasy about where the profession is headed. Microsoft is facing a related tension of its own. The company is sitting on enormous cash reserves built in part by the people it's now cutting loose, as it reshapes its workforce around AI investment.

Linus Torvalds, the creator of Linux, says the kernel project has moved past what he calls its stabilization phase. He says the project is now using a healthier model for handling AI-generated code contributions. Still, he notes that machine-written patches often need to be rewritten by human developers before they're usable.

India is floating a new approach to artificial intelligence policy, one built around a simple principle: the model should travel to the data, not the other way around. The idea is to expand local AI development without handing big technology companies open access to India's data.

Google says Asian advertisers are spending less money, and it points to customs changes in Europe as the reason. The company flagged the trend alongside a separate report that brake pedals have been failing on cars made by Huawei, the Chinese technology company.

NASA is looking to the commercial space industry for help saving the Hubble Space Telescope. Hubble isn't in any immediate danger, but its orbit is slowly decaying, and without a boost, it's expected to fall from the sky sometime in the early to mid 2030s. The agency is now taking proposals from private companies on how to extend its life.

Back on Earth, a California ballot measure is drawing scrutiny from environmental advocates. Proposition 45 is pitched as a way to fast-track approval for essential infrastructure projects by shortening environmental review timelines. Critics warn it could also create a loophole letting data center developers sidestep public scrutiny and avoid legal challenges from residents.

In smart home news, leaked code suggests Apple may be adding security camera features to its HomeView smart home hub. A Reddit user found strings describing motion detection, live streaming, recording, and privacy controls, along with what looks like a dedicated setup page.

And Jackery, the portable power company, has unveiled its smallest power station yet. The new Explorer 100D uses the same lithium iron phosphate batteries Jackery introduced back in 2023, but this version measures just four-point-six by three-point-four by three-point-four inches and weighs under two pounds.

## the NFL

Six straight losses now for Philadelphia, and the bottom is starting to feel real. The Eagles built a 10-0 lead on the Commanders Sunday night, set up by a Saquon Barkley touchdown off a Washington fumble. Then Jayden Daniels and the Commanders answered with 27 unanswered points, and Philadelphia fell 27-10 in Landover. Daniels threw for 217 yards and a touchdown as Washington, previously a one-win team, ran away with it in the second half.

Head coach Nick Sirianni took responsibility afterward, saying flatly that he isn't doing a good enough job and has to do better. The Eagles are now 2-6, their worst stretch since Andy Reid's final season in 2012, when Philadelphia also dropped six in a row. The offense managed just 10 points on the night, and the losing streak has the team projected into top-five territory for the 2027 draft.

Saquon Barkley did address his own future. He told reporters after the game that he's received assurances from the front office that he won't be traded before the November 10th deadline, and said he wants to stay and help fix the season rather than leave. The Eagles were missing several players against Washington, including DeVonta Smith, Jihaad Campbell, and Dontayvion Wicks, all ruled out on the final injury report.

Looking ahead, Philadelphia now heads into a Week 9 matchup against the Giants as home underdogs, according to FanDuel. Worth noting, the Eagles have never won a home game as underdogs under Sirianni, a streak that goes back to his hiring in 2021.

Elsewhere around the league, Sunday brought its share of chaos. ## the WNBA

The Golden State Valkyries are WNBA champions, and they did it faster than almost anyone thought possible. Golden State beat the Atlanta Dream 68 to 63 in Game 7 on Saturday, capturing the title in just the franchise's second season. When the team joined the league, its owner predicted a championship within five years. It took two.

Gabby Williams made sure of it. She scored 30 points in the deciding game, including 28 in the second half alone, and was named Finals MVP. Her performance anchored a Valkyries team that insiders say simply refused to doubt itself through a seven-game series.

For Atlanta, the loss stings, but the season still marked a breakthrough for the Dream. Angel Reese, the Dream forward, said she's confident the team will contend for titles down the road, telling reporters that falling short this time doesn't mean the group won't be champions eventually.

Away from the Finals, the Indiana Fever made a front-office move, hiring Sheri Sam as assistant general manager. Sam previously worked as scouting manager for the NBA's Trail Blazers.

The league also set a date for its next draft lottery. Houston, Toronto, Washington, Seattle, Portland, and Los Angeles will take part in the six-team lottery for the 2027 draft, with the broadcast set for November 15th on ESPN.
