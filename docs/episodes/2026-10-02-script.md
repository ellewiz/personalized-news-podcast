## Markets

Bond markets across Europe are the story investors can't look away from right now. A selloff in global government debt has hit France harder and faster than almost anyone expected. Traders are growing nervous about missed deficit targets and political gridlock in Paris, and that unease is pushing French bond yields sharply higher. Kim Crawford, a global fixed income portfolio manager at JPMorgan Asset Management, told Bloomberg Television that bonds typically sell off until something breaks, and her view is that something already has. The pressure isn't confined to France. The UK is still paying a steep price in its own bond market, with lingering skepticism about British government finances adding billions in extra borrowing costs. And at the European Central Bank, the bond-spread blowout has actually started easing within Friday's session, as traders now bet the central bank will deliver fewer rate hikes to avoid further strain on its more indebted members. European markets are open and trading through all of this as the session continues.

That same spike in interest rates is doing real damage on the equity side. Smaller stocks, bank shares, and utilities have taken the brunt of the selling, since those sectors are especially sensitive to higher borrowing costs and bond yields pulling investment away from riskier, rate-sensitive names. Michael Hartnett, a strategist at Bank of America, says investors are likely to keep avoiding riskier trades until the dollar's recent surge shows signs of topping out. A stronger dollar makes it harder for the kind of smaller, debt-heavy companies feeling this pain to compete and borrow cheaply, so until that reverses, Hartnett argues the risk-off mood will stick around.

All of this sets up the US jobs report as the next major test, with the data due out after this broadcast and the US market still to open for the day. Economists expect nonfarm payrolls rose by ninety thousand jobs last month, following a stronger August gain that was the best since March. That number matters because resilient hiring has been giving the Federal Reserve room to keep fighting inflation without worrying about the labor market cracking. Gold held steady ahead of the release, as traders waited to see whether the data shifts the odds on an October rate move. Stocks were broadly higher in a calmer session overnight, a pause after a week in which sharp swings in bond yields rattled markets, with everyone now waiting to see whether the payrolls number confirms that calm or reignites the selling.
