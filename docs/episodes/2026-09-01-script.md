## New Jersey Politics

Congressional debates in New Jersey are becoming harder to find, and that includes the race in your own backyard. NJ Spotlight News and the New Jersey Monitor both report that candidates for the state's House seats are increasingly skipping the League of Women Voters' public forums, a tradition that's been a mainstay of the state's elections for decades. Experts quoted in the reporting say ducking these forums cuts down the risk of an on-stage gaffe going viral, especially now that campaigns run more through social media and paid ads than through television. With NJ-12 among the districts watching a competitive cycle, that trend means voters here may get fewer chances to see candidates questioned side by side before they cast a ballot.

Also from NJ Spotlight News, New Jersey's Teacher of the Year, Carla Ferriera, a Newark native, is speaking out about what she calls a culture of fear in schools. In a new interview, Ferriera says she's built what she describes as a safe zone for students and wants state policymakers to pay closer attention to two things. First, ICE operations happening near school grounds. Second, the rapid rise of artificial intelligence in classrooms. Her comments land as a window into concerns teachers across New Jersey, not just in Newark, say they're grappling with this year.

And New Jersey's attorney general has joined a federal lawsuit against Amazon, the online retail giant. The suit, brought alongside federal regulators, alleges Amazon spent years inflating the auction prices that businesses pay to place ads inside its online storefront. Amazon denies the claim. New Jersey's involvement puts the state among those seeking damages or changes to how Amazon prices ad placements for the small and mid-sized businesses that rely on its platform to reach customers.

## Markets

Oil tankers coming under fire in the Strait of Hormuz are rattling markets well beyond the Middle East this morning. Two supertankers trying to exit the strait were struck by projectiles late Monday, according to the maritime security firm Marisks. That's pushed crude prices higher for a second straight session, and it's stoking fears that inflation could reaccelerate just as central banks weigh their next move on interest rates.

That oil spike is a big part of why government bond yields have climbed to their highest level since 2008. In London, where markets were closed Monday for a holiday, gilts led the selloff as traders played catch-up during Tuesday's session, which is currently underway. The pressure has spread to emerging-market bonds too, with the prospect of a Federal Reserve rate hike this month now outweighing concerns about the Iran conflict as the dominant force in trading. Gold, meanwhile, dropped to its lowest level in almost two weeks, since higher rate expectations make it less attractive to hold an asset that pays no yield. Adding to the pressure on rates, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has stepped up his public push for the Bank of Japan to raise borrowing costs further, after Japan's ten-year bond yield touched three percent for the first time since 1996 and the yen kept weakening. With all of this in play, US index futures are pointing to a weaker open once trading begins later this morning.

In deal news, H.I.G. Capital is exploring options for its stake in Rising Pharmaceuticals, the US drugmaker, and a sale is among the possibilities, according to people familiar with the matter. The stake is valued at around one billion dollars. No timeline for a decision has been reported yet.

And Elliott Investment Management, the activist hedge fund run by Paul Singer, has built a stake in Air Liquide, the French industrial gas supplier, according to people familiar with the matter. Bloomberg reports Elliott is pushing the company to improve its profit margins. Representatives for both Elliott and Air Liquide declined to comment on the stake.

## Tech and AI

Today marks the end of an era at Apple. Tim Cook has stepped down as CEO, wrapping up a run that started back in 2011 when he took over from Steve Jobs. In a farewell message to staff, Cook looked back on building Apple into one of the most dominant companies in the world. Alongside that transition, longtime Apple executive Phil Schiller is stepping back from his roles overseeing the App Store and Apple's big product events. Schiller keeps his Apple Fellow title, and Bloomberg reports he'll continue on unspecified projects inside the company.

Amazon is now facing a major legal fight of its own. The Federal Trade Commission and attorneys general from 22 states are suing the company, accusing it of running what they call a secret ad surcharge. FTC chairman Andrew Ferguson says Amazon quietly built higher advertising costs into its platform and passed much of that expense on to shoppers through higher prices.

In the chip world, Nvidia is expanding its reach well past its own hardware. The Register reports Nvidia is building out a licensing business around NVLink, the high-speed connection technology that links its processors together. The upshot is that competitors building systems meant to rival Nvidia may still end up paying Nvidia licensing fees to do it.

A new workplace survey suggests generative AI has spread everywhere, but not very deeply. The research finds AI tools now touch roughly 80 percent of job categories. But within most of those occupations, fewer than half of workers are actually using the tools regularly. Wide adoption, in other words, without much daily reliance yet.

Healthcare cybersecurity had a rough stretch. Pharmaceutical distributor McKesson has confirmed a data breach touching millions of patient records, and the hacking group ShinyHunters is demanding 55.2 million dollars. Separately, security researchers are flagging attacks that have reached even pacemakers, underscoring how exposed medical devices and health data have become.

Anthropic, the AI company behind Claude, is dealing with account security problems of its own. The company says commodity malware is stealing login sessions from paid users, letting attackers freeload on their AI subscriptions by mining tokens on someone else's dime. Anthropic is now cracking down on those hijacked accounts. The company is also facing scrutiny on another front. Internal staff chats where Anthropic employees praised piracy sites have surfaced in a lawsuit from Sony, which argues the company's training practices shortchanged songwriters as AI-generated music climbs the charts.

Regulators in Europe are tightening the net further. ChatGPT and Reddit now fall under the European Union's strictest online safety rules, a shift that comes as both platforms have grown rapidly and now face new compliance obligations around content and user protection.

On a lighter note, Google Maps beat the U.S. government to a symbolic name change. The app has already relabeled Lake Ontario as "Lake America" on its maps, while official federal maps are still catching up.

Bill Gates, the Microsoft co-founder, is getting some unsolicited help thinking through artificial intelligence's impact on the next generation. A teenager's take on preparing students for an AI-driven world is making the rounds, arguing that rethinking education is the clearest path to staying in control as the technology advances.

Messi had said the decision was painful, but that he felt the time had come to step aside from the national side, even as he continues playing for Inter Miami in MLS.

Finally, Joshua Kushner, the venture capitalist, said he regrets ever getting involved in a scrapped plan to sell off stakes in the World Cup itself. Kushner had been part of discussions with FIFA president Gianni Infantino about selling ownership shares in the tournament.
