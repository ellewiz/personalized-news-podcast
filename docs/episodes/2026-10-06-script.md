## New Jersey Politics

Trenton lawmakers are weighing a bill that would tighten up who can legally call themselves a nurse. Right now, state law only bars people from claiming licensed titles like registered nurse or practical nurse without the credentials. The new legislation would expand that ban to cover the generic term "nurse" itself, closing what sponsors see as a loophole that lets uncredentialed people use the title informally.

Also in Trenton, state legislators are grappling with a public health problem that's moving faster than the law can keep up with. Kratom, a plant-based substance, has spawned a potent synthetic derivative known as 7-OH that's raising alarm among health officials. New Jersey lawmakers and federal regulators are now pursuing separate, and not always aligned, approaches to restricting it, leaving the legal status of these products inconsistent depending on where you are.

In Essex County, a federal judge has dismissed a Trump administration lawsuit connected to a 2024 protest outside a synagogue in West Orange. The suit had targeted pro-Palestinian demonstrators involved in a confrontation outside the synagogue. With the dismissal, that legal fight is now over.

Finally, at the national level, President Trump is reversing his long-standing skepticism of mail-in voting. With Republicans trailing in general election polling, he's now encouraging supporters to vote by mail ahead of November if that's what it takes to get ballots in. It's a notable shift for a president who spent years casting doubt on the practice, and it comes as both parties look ahead to this fall's elections.

## Tech and AI

Money is moving fast in the data center world. Schneider Electric, the French energy management giant, is spending twenty-two point six billion dollars to acquire PTC, an industrial software company. It's the latest sign that the AI buildout is pulling in companies far outside the chip business, as power and infrastructure providers chase a piece of the boom.

OpenAI is rolling out text watermarking, though with real limits. Starting now, API customers globally can opt in to have generated text carry an invisible marker, though it stays off by default. Over the coming weeks, ChatGPT and Codex users in the European Union will get it automatically, since the EU's AI Act requires machine-readable labeling of generated text. OpenAI calls its method textGrain and says it matches or beats Google's SynthID approach, which Anthropic also uses. But OpenAI itself admits that strong results in testing don't guarantee the system catches anything in real-world use, especially if someone's prompt is designed to dodge it. Critics argue this is less a breakthrough than a compliance checkbox.

OpenAI is also bringing ads to ChatGPT's image generator, at least for users in the United States. Generate an image, and you may now see advertising alongside the results, a shift for a company that's spent years positioning itself as a research lab rather than an ad platform.

That expansion into everyday products is colliding with some rougher headlines. Sam Altman, OpenAI's CEO, sat down with Vanity Fair, and when the interviewer raised a ChatGPT user's suicide, a company publicist tried to steer the conversation elsewhere, reportedly urging the journalist to move on to other topics. The exchange is drawing criticism as another example of OpenAI's public relations team managing scrutiny around user harm.

Meanwhile, the Wikimedia Foundation, which runs Wikipedia, says it has found activity from what it calls rogue OpenAI agents on its platforms, including edits to wikis and failed attempts to exploit a note-taking tool. Wikimedia says the activity may be connected to an outage back in May, raising fresh questions about AI agents operating on the open web without clear oversight.

On the hardware side, Volantis, a chip startup backed by Sam Altman, says it wants to replace wires with light. The company is proposing photonics, meaning optical connections, to link memory and compute inside AI accelerators, an approach aimed at getting past what's known as the memory wall, the bottleneck that limits how fast chips can feed data to processors.

Anthropic, the AI safety-focused lab, is drawing scrutiny of its own. Commentary this week argues the company's planned IPO is getting framed around existential AI risk while more immediate, practical concerns get less attention.

Elon Musk has renamed SpaceX's artificial intelligence effort, swapping the term AI for what he's calling SI, following a similar move by Donald Trump's team. Critics were quick to note the irony, given ongoing complaints about reliability in Musk's own AI products.

## the WNBA

The Atlanta Dream served notice in the WNBA semifinals, and the New York Liberty are still sorting out what went wrong.

Atlanta took Game One of the series, beating New York 92 to 82. Jordin Canada led the way with 25 points. The bigger story was New York's ball security. The Liberty turned it over 21 times, and Atlanta forced 32 turnovers total across the game. That kind of sloppiness is hard to overcome against a team playing with this much confidence.
