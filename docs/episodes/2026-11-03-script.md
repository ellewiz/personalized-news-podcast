## Markets

Chinese policymakers spent part of Tuesday trying to prop up the yuan and the country's stock market, and it didn't go well. Beijing's stimulus push fell flat, according to Bloomberg reporting, even as the People's Bank of China moved to cool the currency's rally by telling banks to hold more foreign reserves. That step actually broke the onshore yuan's winning streak, the longest one in three decades, snapping it just as officials were also trying to keep equities afloat. Compounding the pressure, a major Chinese conglomerate called Huafa is sitting on sixty-two billion dollars in debt strain, and the fallout triggered the worst selloff in China's credit market in two years. That's rattling a core assumption in Chinese finance, that state ties guarantee a backstop for struggling firms. All of this happened during Tuesday's Asian session, which wrapped hours ago.

Oil is the story to watch as US trading gets underway. Crude rose early Tuesday, clawing back part of a steep drop from the prior session, after the United States struck Iran's Qeshm Island. The two sides remain far apart on reopening the Strait of Hormuz, the chokepoint that much of the world's oil trade depends on. Gold, meanwhile, slid after that oil rebound, giving back gains it had just made, as traders bet the US and Iran are nowhere close to a deal. Energy traders are already adjusting. BGN, a commodity trading firm, says it's buying more tankers, including very large crude carriers, to keep moving supply through the disruption. Argentina's YPF is also trying to lock in energy security, with CEO Horacio Marin saying the company hopes to sign a deal with Eni and XRG by the end of November.

Finally, a major real estate move out of the Gulf. Dubai's Emaar Properties is reportedly studying a buyout of its own construction arm, a listed unit valued at fourteen billion dollars, according to people familiar with the matter. No price tag or timeline has been confirmed yet. Separately in the UK, Brookfield Asset Management and the Qatar Investment Authority have agreed to put two and a half billion pounds, worth about three and a half billion dollars, into a data center project with the power generator Drax Group. Both deals point to the same theme pulling capital right now: infrastructure and real assets, even as broader credit markets, especially in China, look shakier by the day.

## Tech and AI

Apple dominated the tech headlines, with the company posting record fourth-quarter results. Total revenue came in at 114.5 billion dollars, up 12 percent from the same quarter a year earlier. iPhone revenue jumped 17.5 percent to 57.58 billion dollars, a record, even though Apple didn't release a new standard iPhone model during this refresh cycle. Mac revenue climbed nearly 24 percent. Services grew close to 10 percent. Wearables and iPad sales both declined. This was John Ternus's first earnings call as CEO, and he was asked whether Apple plans to merge macOS and iOS into a single operating system. He said Apple has no fundamentally different changes planned right now. Separately, Ternus told Reuters that Apple still can't predict when it will have enough iPhone Duo units to meet demand, saying only that the company is working to get the device to customers as quickly as possible. Also from Apple, Ars Technica reports the company is working on a home robot, backed by Ternus, aimed at tasks like FaceTime calls and controlling smart lights, expected in 2027 with a price tag around 1,000 dollars.

In the AI industry, Anthropic's finances are coming into sharper focus through its IPO filing. The company's compute spending commitments could eventually top one trillion dollars. Third-quarter revenue is nearing 17 billion dollars, but the filing warns that the twelve-fold revenue growth Anthropic has seen won't continue at that pace. The Register also notes the filing is heavy on promises but light on answers to harder questions about the business. Meanwhile, Anthropic has updated its Claude Code tool to stop it from being used to recreate existing video games, after a wave of AI-generated clones of retro titles. Ars Technica points out this move is unlikely to slow the broader trend, since the volume of AI-made game copies now goes well beyond what any single company can contain.

On the infrastructure side, Oracle has already borrowed up to the full 20 billion dollar credit limit it set for itself just a week earlier, as the company pours money into data center expansion. It says it won't borrow further through the end of 2030. OpenAI, for its part, announced a pilot program it's calling Grid Protectors, starting at a data center in Effingham County, Georgia. The company says it will free up to 1,000 megawatts of power during extreme weather or emergencies, enough to supply air conditioning to roughly 700,000 homes, an effort that comes as local opposition grows to the energy demands of new data centers. And Marvell, the chipmaker, is betting 115 billion dollars on a photonics company as it pushes further into optical networking, anticipating a surge in demand for optical systems as Nvidia and others build bigger AI infrastructure.

In security news, Google says one of its Android hardware partners, a Shenzhen-based manufacturer called Videostrong Technology, built a hidden backdoor into 1.3 million set-top boxes and turned them into a botnet. Google has dismantled the backdoor and banned the company from making or selling certified Android devices. Separately, the FBI has formally attributed the 6.3 billion dollar crypto heist from Binance to North Korean hackers. Binance is working to replace the stolen funds as customers pull money from the exchange, while the hackers move to launder what they took.

Finally, a few smaller items worth a mention. Raspberry Pi has raised prices again, barely a month after its last increase, with a memory shortage pushing the 16 gigabyte Pi 5 to 385 dollars, more than triple its original launch price. And SUSE, the open source software company, says it's cutting about one in ten of its employees, with its CEO pointing directly to changes driven by AI tooling in its daily workflows.
