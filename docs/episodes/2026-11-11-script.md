## New Jersey Politics

New Jersey's exit polls from last week's midterms are still being picked apart, and one trend stands out. NJ Spotlight News reports Asian American voters across the state broke clearly for Democrats. That finding adds another data point to how last week's results broke down by community, on top of the overall numbers already being analyzed statewide.

Governor Mikie Sherrill has signed a new executive order aimed at making New Jersey more attractive to businesses. The order directs her top deputies to study changes to taxes, fees, and licensing rules. It also calls for targeted state investment in select industry sectors. The specifics of which taxes or fees could change are still to be worked out by Sherrill's administration.

New Jersey is also getting federal money for rural health care, though not much of it. The state will receive 142 million dollars from a 10 billion dollar federal grant program Congress created to help rural health systems absorb Medicaid cuts. That 142 million dollar figure is the lowest amount awarded to any state in the country.

On a public health note, NJ Spotlight News ran an op-ed warning that measles is making a comeback nationally, and asking whether New Jersey is prepared. The piece points to declining vaccination rates as the reason outbreaks are spreading, and argues the state could lose protections it's built up over years if that trend continues.

Shifting to Washington, a few federal stories touch New Jersey only through its congressional delegation, which now has to navigate a changed Capitol Hill. New member orientation included what's being called an office lottery, as the parties settle into new space after the midterms. Ohio Congressman Jim Jordan was elected by House Republicans to lead the party in the next Congress, following the GOP's losses last week. South Dakota Senator John Thune was reelected as Senate Republican leader, even though Republicans lost their majority in that chamber this cycle. And the House blocked an effort to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, over charges he faces, after a vote Tuesday. None of those four involve New Jersey state government directly, but they'll shape how the state's House and Senate members operate for the next two years.

## Markets

Markets got a jolt from two directions overnight, and futures are showing it clearly before the opening bell.

Start with inflation and the Federal Reserve. A hotter-than-expected US inflation reading has traders betting on more aggressive rate hikes from the Fed, and Treasury futures fell on the news as yields moved higher. Gold initially dropped on that same inflation print and hawkish comments from Fed officials, but it's since edged back up as traders reassess the odds of a December hike. The net effect: borrowing costs look set to stay elevated longer than markets had hoped just a few weeks ago.

But the bigger story for equities is artificial intelligence, and it's a sharp reversal from the gloom that hit hedge funds through October. US stock futures are rallying after CoreWeave, the cloud computing firm focused on AI workloads, posted strong results that eased concerns about overbuilding in the sector. At the same time, demand for Anthropic's initial public offering is coming in robust, and that IPO is being watched as a bellwether for whether the broader AI trade still has legs after a rough stretch. Hedge funds took a beating in October as the AI trade wobbled, and this rebound in futures suggests some of that anxiety is fading, at least for now. Not every AI name is riding the wave, though. Nvidia shares have been under pressure amid an internal investigation into CEO Jensen Huang over an alleged relationship with a Chinese spy, a story that's raising real questions about the company's leadership even as the rest of the sector rallies.

Overseas, Europe's session is underway and mixed. Vestas, the Danish wind turbine maker, saw its shares fall after missing third-quarter revenue estimates. And CSG, a European arms manufacturer, saw its stock plunge to its lowest level since its listing after cutting its full-year profit outlook, a blow to investors who'd bet on defense spending growth. Asian markets, meanwhile, wrapped up their session hours ago, with China continuing a busy year of offshore fundraising, planning a two-billion-dollar dollar-denominated bond sale in Hong Kong.

## Tech and AI

The biggest Nvidia story of the week isn't about chips at all. Bloomberg and the New York Times both report that Nvidia gave federal investigators an incomplete picture of its business with Megaspeed, a Singapore-based data center company under suspicion of smuggling Nvidia's AI chips into China. According to trade records cited by Bloomberg, Nvidia undercounted the chips it sold to Megaspeed through hardware partners by fifty thousand units, worth billions of dollars. The Times reports the Commerce Department investigation into Megaspeed was abruptly transferred to the Justice Department after Nvidia's general counsel, Tim Teter, contacted investigators referencing a Trump-appointed Commerce official, Jeff Kessler, as having authorized the outreach. Some officials reportedly viewed that contact as a breach of procedure that may have tainted the case. The Megaspeed executive at the center of this is believed by U.S. officials to be a Chinese intelligence asset who also had a personal relationship with Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang. That relationship, and Nvidia's handling of the smuggling probe, are now feeding a broader question about succession at the company. Ars Technica reports Nvidia's board is probing the matter, and investors are increasingly uneasy that the company has no clear plan for who would run Nvidia without Huang.

Elsewhere in AI, Anthropic's planned IPO is drawing public skepticism. The cognitive scientist Gary Marcus argued the company is spending far more than it earns and said he wouldn't be shocked if Anthropic goes out of business or if the IPO itself fails. He compared the company's capital needs to WeWork's failed IPO attempts.

Anthropic's Claude is also expanding its reach inside Microsoft's ecosystem. Microsoft is now giving Claude default access to UK Copilot tenants, though administrators have until December 9th to review those access settings, since Anthropic processes that data in the United States rather than domestically. Separately, Anthropic added new Sheets, Whiteboards, and Data artifact types to Claude, pushing the assistant further into territory long held by Microsoft Office.

OpenAI, meanwhile, says it's built a kill switch for its research models, designed to shut down a rogue AI agent in as little as thirty-five seconds. And in Australia, regulators are considering making it a crime to knowingly sell unsafe AI systems, with a new regulator that would require developers to test their models and allow outside inspection.

Google's DeepMind unit had two notable moves. It unveiled Gemini Robotics 2.5, a new robotics model the company says is up to twice as successful at certain tasks as its predecessor, alongside an open safety benchmark called ASIMOV-jailbreak. DeepMind also worked with outside researchers to formally vet the security of Signal's encrypted messaging technology, specifically its post-quantum handshake and Triple Ratchet protocol.

On the hardware side, AMD laid out its next generation of server chips. The company detailed Zen 6 cores capable of up to two hundred fifty-six cores and clock speeds up to five-point-three gigahertz, with performance gains between 20 and 31 percent depending on configuration. AMD also revived its Epyc X-chip line after a three-year gap, aimed at engineering and high-performance computing workloads, and detailed Verano, its first step toward a Zen-based chip meant to compete with Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform in AI data centers.

Amazon is pushing its Ring security brand into robotics. The company unveiled an AI-powered home security drone and a patrol robot, both described as "proactive protection" devices, with plans to launch them in late 2026 and early 2027.

In wearables, Oura, the maker of the health-tracking ring, announced it's acquiring Counsel Health, an AI chatbot startup that bills itself as an AI-native primary care company, for six hundred twenty-five million dollars. Oura had already built Counsel Health's chatbot into its app earlier this year.

On the security front, a researcher found that thirty-five thousand projects built with Grok's coding agent, called Grok Build, were left exposed online, leaking confidential information including some belonging to SpaceX. Separately, Fireworks AI, an inference company, disclosed a second data exposure, which it attributed to its cloud provider and to so-called LLMjacking attackers exploiting leaked credentials.

And Stanford researchers published a five-year review of artificial intelligence's impact on work, science, and public life. The study's own authors concede it's already out of date, with little consensus on who, if anyone, is actually in control of the technology's trajectory.

## Soccer and the World Cup

The biggest storyline hanging over the US national team right now isn't even happening on American soil. It's in Manchester.

Mauricio Pochettino, the USMNT head coach, is reportedly open to taking over at Manchester United, according to sources who spoke with ESPN. United's current head coach, Michael Carrick, is still leading training as the club waits on a decision, and United's leadership is set to meet in Monaco to settle Carrick's future. ESPN reports the club has already been in contact with Pochettino directly.

For now, Pochettino remains the USMNT boss, and his team is in the middle of preparing for the Concacaf Nations League. Defender Antonee Robinson said the speculation hasn't thrown off the group's preparations, telling reporters the players aren't distracted by the Manchester United chatter. Whether that holds if an offer actually materializes is another question, and it's one that could reshape the program with a World Cup year bearing down on the US.

Meanwhile in World Cup follow-up news, Scotland midfielder Scott McTominay revealed he needed his heart shocked back into rhythm just two days before playing in Scotland's World Cup opener against Haiti. He called the situation "not ideal." Scotland head coach Sebastien Pocognoli has since welcomed McTominay back into the squad, praising him as one of the leaders who helped convince Pocognoli to take the job.

Elsewhere on the international front, France will be without Marcus Thuram, the Inter Milan forward, for their Nations League matches due to a hamstring injury. His teammate Aurelien Tchouameni is also out injured. And in goalkeeping news, BBC Sport's chief football writer raised the question of whether England's Jordan Pickford is finally facing real competition for his starting spot.

## the NFL

The trade deadline came and went on Tuesday, and the Eagles were busier than most people expected, even if one of their moves didn't survive the day.

Start with Arnold Ebiketie. The Eagles agreed to send the edge rusher to the Bengals for a 2027 sixth-round pick. Cincinnati had signed off on the deal, but about an hour later, the Bengals canceled it. They'd reconsidered how trading for Ebiketie would affect their own compensatory draft pick formula next year. So for now, Ebiketie stays an Eagle.

Philadelphia also waived running back Jaydon Blue. It's the second time they've let him go. Blue had been the third option in the backfield behind Saquon Barkley and Elijah Mitchell, and he'd mostly been used on kick returns rather than on offense.

Meanwhile, head coach Nick Sirianni spent part of his week denying a gesture. Video circulated of Sirianni leaving the field after Sunday's loss to the Giants, Philadelphia's seventh straight defeat, alongside team security chief Dom DiSandro. Sirianni says it wasn't what it looked like, pushing back on the idea that he flashed a middle finger toward fans.

The seven-game skid is the backdrop for all of this. A team that opened the season with real expectations is now searching for answers, and both the roster moves and the sideline scrutiny reflect a franchise trying to find its footing at the midpoint of the year.

Beyond Philadelphia, deadline day reshaped several quarterback rooms around the league. The Raiders traded Kirk Cousins back to the Vikings, sending a 2027 third-round pick to Minnesota in return. It's a reunion for Cousins, who started his career there before leaving for Atlanta and then Las Vegas. The move comes after an offensive performance so poor against the Bills that it pushed the Vikings into the market immediately. With Cousins gone, the Raiders turned the keys over to rookie Fernando Mendoza, their first overall pick, officially naming him the starter. Las Vegas's general manager said Mendoza had passed every test they put in front of him.

The wide receiver market moved too. The Chiefs traded for Josh Downs from the Colts, adding speed and route-running ability to an offense that's needed exactly that around Patrick Mahomes. On the same day, Mahomes and his wife Brittany announced they're expecting their fourth child. The 49ers also dipped into Indianapolis's roster, acquiring Keenan Allen to help a receiver group that's been hit hard by injuries. And the Bills addressed their secondary, trading a second-round pick to the Titans for safety Amani Hooker, along with a fifth-round pick coming back to Buffalo.

Not everyone moved. The Colts fielded trade interest in both running back Jonathan Taylor and quarterback Daniel Jones, but held onto both as the deadline passed. The Chargers, despite plenty of speculation, didn't make a notable addition either.

Elsewhere around the league, the Commanders are stuck in a frustrating pattern. Owner, coaches, and players all share the same sense of déjà vu after another close loss, with head coach Dan Quinn acknowledging how repetitive it's become to describe Washington as a team that's almost there but isn't.

In Pittsburgh, Aaron Rodgers is one win away from a piece of NFL history. His next victory will give him 181 career wins counting playoffs, tying him with Drew Brees for fourth all-time.

And in New York, Giants receiver Malik Nabers pushed back hard against criticism from a Fox broadcast crew. Nabers said commentary questioning his body language and effort during Sunday's game was simply untrue, calling it a lie and saying the broadcast shouldn't tell the world something that isn't him.
