## New Jersey Politics

New Jersey's congressional map looks a lot different this morning than it did a day earlier. Democrats flipped two of the state's three Republican-held House seats, a result largely read as a rebuke of President Trump.

The biggest shock came in the 2nd District, where Cape May City Mayor Zack Mullock, a Democrat, ousted longtime Republican Rep. Jeff Van Drew. Van Drew has conceded. In the 7th District, Democrat Rebecca Bennett defeated Republican Rep. Tom Kean Junior, who had held the seat for four years. Bennett celebrated with supporters in Bridgewater, telling the crowd voters can soon move past the Trump era.

For listeners in Ewing and Mercer County, the seat that matters most, New Jersey's 12th District, stays Democratic. Plastic surgeon Adam Hamawy won the heavily Democratic district, succeeding retiring Rep. Bonnie Watson Coleman.

Elsewhere on the delegation, incumbents largely held their ground. Rep. Nellie Pou won her first full term in the 9th District, a seat Trump carried in 2024. Rep. Chris Smith won a 24th term, extending a run in Congress of more than 45 years. Rep. Josh Gottheimer was reelected to a sixth term. Rep. Frank Pallone, the most senior Democrat in the state's delegation, won a 20th term. Rep. Herb Conaway, a doctor who spent 27 years in the state Assembly before moving to Washington, won his second term. Rep. Donald Norcross returns for a seventh full term in the 1st District, and Rep. Rob Menendez, who faced no Republican challenger in the Hudson County-based 8th District, won a third term. Rep. LaMonica McIver also won reelection easily in her district. She's set to begin a second full term in January while still fighting federal charges accusing her of assaulting agents outside Newark's migrant detention facility, a case that remains in federal court and separate from her House race.

At the top of the ticket, Sen. Cory Booker, the former Newark mayor, won reelection comfortably and will begin a third full term in January. His win came as Democrats nationally also claimed control of the U.S. Senate, a result that was still coming into focus as votes were counted.

Taken together, the two flipped House seats helped power a broader Democratic sweep. Nationally, Democrats are expected to retake control of the U.S. House once all ballots are counted, a dramatic swing from the current Republican majority.

One data point worth flagging out of this cycle: voter registration among New Jersey residents aged eighteen and nineteen dropped compared with the last midterm election, according to state data. Turnout among the state's youngest eligible voters is something election officials will likely be watching heading into future cycles.

## Markets

The dominant story across every market right now is the Democratic sweep of Congress, and the ripple effects are showing up everywhere from Seoul to the bond market.

Here's the sequence. Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress in the US midterm elections. President Trump said he would accept the results. That combination sent a wave of relief through global markets, because it removed the uncertainty investors hate most, the fear of a contested outcome.

US stock futures rallied hard, reversing earlier losses, as traders bought into the dip from earlier in the week. European stocks, trading now in their mid-session, extended those gains, with renewable energy companies and banks leading the way. Investors are betting a Democrat-controlled Congress means friendlier policy for clean energy, and renewable stocks surged not just in Europe but worldwide. Over in Asia, where markets have already finished their day, the picture was more chaotic. South Korea's benchmark swung through a seven-percentage-point range before closing higher, as worries about the artificial intelligence trade whipsawed sentiment. Singapore's Straits Times Index clawed back from a brief technical correction by the end of its session, even as bank shares there slid.

The dollar took the biggest hit. It sank the most in two months on concern that political gridlock and tougher tech regulation under a Democratic Congress could dampen foreign demand for US assets. That weaker dollar, combined with falling Treasury yields, helped send gold surging above four thousand dollars an ounce. Treasuries themselves rallied, with investors betting that a divided government limits how far the Trump administration can push its tax and spending agenda.

Not every stock benefited. Shares of Elon Musk's Tesla and his rocket company SpaceX fell ahead of Wednesday's US market open, as investors weighed what a Democrat-led Congress means for a major Republican donor's business empire.

Separately, a major pharmaceutical deal is worth flagging. Boehringer Ingelheim, the German drugmaker, signed a licensing agreement with China's Sino Biopharmaceutical and its subsidiaries for experimental cancer treatments. The deal could be worth more than seven billion dollars, making it one of the largest biotech licensing agreements of the year.

## Tech and AI

AMD closed out its quarter with numbers that would have sounded absurd a few years ago. CEO Lisa Su told analysts on Tuesday's earnings call that the third quarter of 2026 was, in her words, the largest and most profitable in company history. Revenue hit thirteen point four billion dollars. AMD says next quarter will be even bigger, with a forecast of seventeen billion dollars, driven by data center and AI chip demand. The company also says its supply chain can keep pace with that growth, and it's now on track to overtake Intel as the biggest x86 chipmaker by volume.

Apple had its own earnings call this week, under new CEO John Ternus, his first as the company's top executive. Commentary afterward focused less on what he said and more on how little of it broke from Apple's usual scripted format. But the underlying numbers were strong. Mac sales are booming, and iPhone sales grew seventeen percent year over year, notable because that growth came without the newest iPhone eighteen or the Duo model shipping during the quarter. The one shadow over both Apple and the broader PC industry is memory. RAM prices are climbing industry-wide, and Microsoft is feeling it too, squeezing margins even as demand for AI-capable hardware stays high. Microsoft has responded by optimizing Windows 11 to run better on systems with just eight gigabytes of memory, while still pushing its AI PC pitch to budget-conscious customers.

Apple also pushed out a wave of developer betas this week. Siri AI is opening up to outside developers for the first time, letting apps integrate directly with it ahead of a planned public release in the spring. Safari is getting a vertical tab layout and an adaptive dark mode for websites that don't support it natively. And reviewers have been testing the new iPad Mini, built around Apple's A20 Pro chip. Early verdicts are mixed but mostly positive. Reviewers praised its new acoustic display speaker system and markedly improved battery life, though several noted the screen still caps out at 60 hertz, missing Apple's high-refresh ProMotion technology found in pricier devices.

Over at Nvidia, CEO Jensen Huang made clear the company isn't picking sides in the open-versus-closed AI model debate. Speaking at an event called Fireworks Forge, Huang said Nvidia is happy to sell chips to companies building with open models or closed ones alike, so long as they're buying Nvidia hardware to do it.

Not every AI growth story was celebratory. British fintech Revolut said it cut its AI coding costs by a factor of ten by dropping Anthropic's Claude in favor of self-hosted models, saving enough to let engineers use AI more liberally for everyday debugging work. Meanwhile a group of AI policy researchers warned about what they're calling sovereign agents, AI systems designed to operate outside human control and pursue their own open-ended goals, which the group argues pose a serious risk if left unchecked.

In the business-of-tech column, design software company Canva announced layoffs affecting one thousand two hundred seventy one people, twenty two percent of its staff. CEO and co-founder Melanie Perkins said some remaining employees will be asked to relocate to centralized hubs. In the UK, telecom provider Virgin Media O2 outlined plans to lay off thousands of workers as part of an eight hundred million pound cost-cutting plan through 2029, with three hundred million pounds of that coming directly from staff reductions.

Crypto exchange Binance has published its account of how it lost six point three billion dollars in what's being called the largest crypto theft on record. The company says the breach wasn't a single hack but a monthslong operation, starting when a developer downloaded a tampered software installer spread through a malicious Google ad. Binance says the attackers used fake job interviews to gain further access, alongside an internal AI tool, eventually pulling off the heist.

And in a legal fight with implications across the AI industry, The New York Times and several other publishers have sued the maker of the Brave web browser. The suit accuses Brave of scraping copyrighted news content and selling access to it to AI companies including Anthropic, Perplexity, Mistral, xAI, and Cohere, while also promoting a built-in tool that lets users bypass those same publishers' paywalls.

In Baltimore, the Ravens are down to their third quarterback.
