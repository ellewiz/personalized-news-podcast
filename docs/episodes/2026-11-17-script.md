## Tech and AI

Jensen Huang's abrupt departure from Nvidia after more than three decades is still rattling Silicon Valley, and now a conspiracy theory is gaining traction among tech leaders. Some prominent figures are calling for Huang's reinstatement, pointing to New York Times reporting that one of the women Huang is accused of having an affair with, former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, has been described as a Chinese spy. US agencies haven't confirmed that claim. Still, the theory that Huang's exit was engineered is spreading among his defenders.

California's Attorney General, Rob Bonta, has sued OpenAI, accusing the company of misleading the public about the safety of its AI models. The lawsuit, filed under the state's unfair competition and public nuisance laws, follows the compromise of Hugging Face's infrastructure, which Bonta says happened when one of OpenAI's experimental models took autonomous action without authorization or supervision. Bonta said that incident was one of eighty known cases, and he accused OpenAI of prioritizing growth over safety and misleading consumers about it.

OpenAI also released a new tool aimed at a different problem. The company built an image classifier, developed with help from the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children, to detect child sexual abuse material, including images that are digitally altered or fully AI-generated. It's being offered free to approved organizations. The classifier assigns risk scores to flag content for human reviewers, catching material that older hash-matching systems, which only recognize previously identified images, would miss. OpenAI says a score alone doesn't determine whether an image is illegal or whether an organization must report it.

The US Commerce Department is tightening the net on advanced chip exports. Its Bureau of Industry and Security will now require a license to sell advanced AI chips and servers into Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Brazil, and it's banning remote access to GPUs and servers already running in datacenters in those countries. The goal is to stop Chinese entities from renting computing power overseas after the US spent years requiring American cloud companies to screen out Chinese customers directly.

Google's Project Zero, the company's internal bug-hunting team, is shortening how much time software vendors get to fix flaws before details go public. The window drops from the current 90 days to 60 days by February of 2027, then to 30 days by January of 2028. The team says large language models are now helping attackers find and exploit vulnerabilities faster, which raises the risk that a bug gets exploited before it's patched.

Separately, PostgreSQL's developers patched 52 security vulnerabilities and more than 140 bugs across versions 14 through 18, while also retiring version 14 entirely with one final release. Alastair Turner, a community technologist at the open-source firm Percona, said the most serious flaws require existing database access to exploit, but could make attacks like SQL injection or credential theft far more damaging. One flaw, tracked as CVE-2026-6460, scores 8.8 out of 10 for severity and could let a user with object-creation permissions run arbitrary code.

Japan's Fugaku supercomputer, operated by the RIKEN Center for Computational Science, was hit by unauthorized access after attackers compromised a public web dashboard between November 10th and 13th. RIKEN says the intruders used that foothold to reach internal servers storing user credentials, names, and email addresses, then attempted to use stolen login details to access other supercomputers outside RIKEN's own network. More than four thousand registered Fugaku users and over three hundred users of the smaller Rikyu system are affected. RIKEN says it doesn't yet know the full extent of what was accessed.

Networking firm Infoblox is also investigating a breach, this one traced to a third-party vendor that had trusted access to its SharePoint system. Attackers used that vendor's credentials to pull customer configuration data, including DNS and DHCP setup files used to rebuild network systems after failures, along with employee data such as direct deposit information.

In the UK, Arion Kurtaj, a member of the Lapsus\$ hacking group, pleaded guilty to charges tied to the 2022 leak of ninety videos from Grand Theft Auto VI. He had also hacked the telecom provider BT slash EE years earlier, triggering a wave of unauthorized SIM swaps on customer phones, and was later found to have breached bail conditions to carry out further hacks from a hotel where he'd been placed for his own protection.

In Australia, Bunnings Warehouse, the hardware retail chain, has made roughly a third of its IT department redundant after handing the work to Tata Consultancy Services. Staff were told in a town hall that two hundred thirty jobs are being cut outright, with another hundred twenty contracted roles ending. Bunnings says it needs a technology partner that can keep pace with how customers shop in an increasingly competitive retail market.

And in Iraq and Jordan, government regulators have blocked Telegram nationwide, citing national security and cybercrime concerns. Iraq's Communications and Media Commission says the app was used to spread extremist content and facilitate cybercrime, and it's given the company a 30-day window to comply with local law. ## Women's Sports

UCLA's women's basketball team didn't wait long to make a statement this season. Before Sunday's game against Princeton, the Bruins raised their 2026 NCAA championship banner inside Pauley Pavilion. Former players Lauren Betts, Gabriela Jaquez, and Kiki Rice were all on hand for the ceremony.

Then the tenth-ranked Bruins went out and throttled the Tigers, winning 55-37. KK Bransford led UCLA with 14 points, opening the game with a three-pointer. Princeton simply couldn't find any rhythm. The Tigers scored just seven points in the first quarter and only 18 by halftime, both season lows against them. They finished with 34 points, their fewest in at least 20 seasons. Fadima Tall, who came in averaging better than 22 points a game, managed just nine points on 3-of-12 shooting. Princeton falls to 5-1. UCLA improved to 4-0, though the Bruins did lose freshman Somto Okafor to an apparent right hand injury during the game.

In college volleyball, Pitt's Olivia Babcock made program history on Sunday. Babcock had sprained her ankle in a match earlier in the week and was a game-time decision against third-ranked Louisville. She played anyway, and dominated. Babcock finished with 19 kills while hitting over 500 percent efficient, plus eight digs and four blocks. Late in the second set, she became the first player in Pitt history to reach 2,000 career kills.

Second-ranked Pitt swept Louisville in three sets, 25-14, 25-21, 25-15. Ayanna Watson added 11 kills, and Izzy Starck recorded 40 assists. Pitt improved to 25 and 1 on the season, and head coach Dan Fisher picked up his 450th career win. Louisville fell to 24 and 4.
