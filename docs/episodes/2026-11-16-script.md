The company says a defect introduced back in July sat dormant until a surge in Teams traffic and a drop in server capacity combined to trigger it. Lock contention inside an internal Windows process exhausted machine resources, and the problem got worse on newer, more powerful hardware, which is the opposite of what you'd expect. The outage ran from November 12th into November 13th, lasting just under a full day. Microsoft says it was not related to any cyberattack or AI system. The fallout is still showing up in strange places. ## Women's News

Childcare turned out to be one of the defining issues of this year's midterms. Nearly every Democrat who flipped a Republican-held seat ran on it. Senator-elect Abdul El-Sayed of Michigan and Representative-elect Melat Kiros of Colorado both campaigned on universal childcare, an idea that was considered politically risky just last year. New Mexico's government has already put a version of it into practice, and New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani built his platform around it too. Elliot Haspel, a child care researcher who's written two books on the subject, says the shift happened because campaigns stopped treating childcare as a niche issue and started folding it into the broader affordability message that drove this election.

That same affordability message showed up again in a separate pattern researchers flagged. The 19th, a nonprofit newsroom, points to Governor Abigail Spanberger of Virginia and Governor Mikie Sherrill of New Jersey as examples of women candidates who ran, and won, on reining in electricity costs tied to data center growth. Sherrill paused her own inauguration to sign two executive orders freezing utility rate hikes and pushing the state's utility board toward solar and other renewable sources. Analysts say rising energy bills and data center buildouts were top concerns for women voters this cycle, and that candidates who addressed them directly outperformed those who didn't.

## the NWSL

The NWSL Championship picture is now set, and it came together through a chaotic semifinal weekend.

The Kansas City Current beat Gotham FC 2-1 to reach the title match in Washington, DC. Gotham had earned home field advantage by finishing first in the league standings, but the club was forced to shift the game to Icahn Stadium on Randall's Island on short notice. Players from Gotham and the Seattle Reign had called the pitch at Sports Illustrated Stadium in Harrison, New Jersey "sandy and uneven" after an earlier quarterfinal there. Icahn Stadium holds fewer than half the fans Gotham usually draws, and the club scrambled to arrange ferries and shuttle buses for supporters. Gotham forward Midge Purce didn't hold back afterward, calling the whole venue situation inexcusable. The loss ends Gotham's bid for a third NWSL title in four years.

The Washington Spirit are the Current's opponent in the championship, after advancing from their own semifinal over the weekend.

Elsewhere in the league, longtime goalkeeper Alyssa Naeher has retired from professional soccer, closing out her career with the Chicago Stars. She'd spent more than fifteen years in the women's game and was known to teammates as "Uncle" for her reliability. Her final two seasons in Chicago were rough, with the Stars finishing last in the league both years. But in 2025 she scored a goal against the Seattle Reign, becoming just the third NWSL goalkeeper ever to find the net.

On the transfer front, the Orlando Pride have signed defender Cori Dyke to a new contract running through the 2029 season. Dyke has been with Orlando since 2024 and won both the NWSL Championship and the NWSL Shield in her rookie year.

And the U.S. women's national team is bringing back its January training camp. ## the WNBA

The 2027 WNBA draft lottery played out on Sunday night, and the Toronto Tempo came away with the top overall pick.

The Washington Mystics landed the No. 3 pick, exactly where their odds pointed. It's the franchise's fifth top-six pick across three draft classes. That pick actually traces back to a 2025 trade, when Washington sent two-time All-Star Ariel Atkins to the Chicago Sky for a first-round pick that became Sonia Citron. The pick swap attached to that deal grew more valuable because of Chicago's two-year record of 26 wins and 62 losses, and it landed in Washington's lap even though the Mystics made the playoffs this past season.

The Portland Fire got the No. 5 pick. Portland had just a five-point-two percent chance at landing the top selection, and ended up exactly where the odds suggested. It'll be the second first-round pick in the expansion franchise's short history, after they took Iyana Martín Carrión out of Spain with the seventh pick a year ago.

Neither the Mystics nor the Fire made representatives available to reporters after the lottery.
