## New Jersey Politics

The prediction markets fight is picking up steam in Trenton. New Jersey is among a group of states pushing back on betting-style prediction platforms like Kalshi and Polymarket, which let users wager on outcomes from elections to sports. State officials are looking at tighter regulation of these sites, arguing they function like unlicensed gambling operations even though they market themselves as financial exchanges. Watch for state regulators and lawmakers to keep pressing the issue in the coming months.

On the federal side, the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services says states now have six extra months to roll out the new Medicaid work requirement included in the Republican tax and spending law passed earlier this year. That provision requires most Medicaid recipients to work, do community service, or attend school for at least eighty hours a month to keep their coverage. Dan Brillman, who directs the agency's Center for Medicaid and CHIP Services, said the extra time will let states lean on existing data to verify work status automatically, rather than forcing people to track down paperwork. That timeline affects New Jersey's own rollout of the requirement for its Medicaid population.

And a Rutgers University analysis is getting attention in state political circles. The Center for American Women and Politics at Rutgers finds that women will hold a record number of state legislative seats nationally following last week's elections, with at least two thousand five hundred fifty-nine women set to serve, nearly thirty-five percent of all state lawmakers. Six state legislatures are projected to reach gender parity of fifty percent or more, though New Jersey isn't among them. The Rutgers center has tracked this data for years as a benchmark for women's political representation, and New Jersey's own legislative makeup will factor into next year's updated count.

## Tech and AI

A rocket failure over the weekend is rippling across the entire space industry, and it's a serious setback for Amazon. United Launch Alliance's Vulcan rocket was carrying forty of Amazon's Leo broadband satellites when it suffered what the company called an anomaly about ten minutes into the flight. Both the rocket and all forty satellites fell back to Earth. This was supposed to be the first of thirty-eight planned Vulcan launches booked by Amazon to build out its answer to SpaceX's Starlink. Amazon's Leo network has already struggled to get a meaningful number of satellites into orbit, and this was the debut flight of a rocket variant custom-built for exactly this mission. Ars Technica reports it's believed to be ULA's first mission failure in its twenty-year history. Vulcan also carries roughly half of the US military's national security launches over the next few years, so expect an extended grounding and review before it flies again. Separately, SpaceX had a much better weekend, successfully catching its Starship upper stage back at the launch tower in Texas, a step the company has been refining as it works toward making the giant rocket rapidly reusable.

The fallout from the Jensen Huang situation at Nvidia keeps widening. The Verge reports that Huang, the company's former CEO, may be trying to find a path back to the top job, and that Megaspeed has issued a statement claiming Alice Huang hasn't actually worked for the company since August of last year. Elon Musk and Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney are among those publicly backing a letter calling for Nvidia to bring Huang back. Meanwhile in Washington, lawmakers from both parties are pushing to tighten export rules on AI chips following the spy scandal tied to Huang. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer has said Huang and the Trump administration owe Congress answers, and Senate Banking Committee Chairman Tim Scott has written to both Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick demanding public testimony.

Microsoft is adjusting cloud prices around the world, and the changes cut in very different directions depending on where customers are. Starting January of 2027, commercial cloud prices in India will rise by nearly sixteen percent, with some on-premises offerings up by as much as twenty percent. New Zealand customers face a ten percent increase. But prices are falling in China, where Microsoft works through local partner 21Vianet, by as much as eight and a half percent, and Australian customers get a four and a half percent cut. Norway sees the steepest reduction anywhere, nearly six percent. Microsoft says the moves track currency exchange rates against its dollar-based pricing. Separately, Microsoft is also overhauling its education pricing, retiring free student email and collaboration services and changing storage limits for schools, a shift the UK's Department for Education has already said it's disappointed by.

Apple is starting a new study aimed at improving AI health tools. The Apple Health AI Usage Study is being run with OpenEvidence, a clinical AI platform already used by healthcare professionals that bases its answers on sources like the New England Journal of Medicine. Participants will use a new iOS app to ask medical questions and then rate the answers for accuracy, detail, and relevance. Enrollment is open now.

And Apple's first smart glasses appear to be taking shape. Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reports the device will skip augmented reality features for now and is expected to be announced at next June's WWDC, with shipments not starting until later in the year. Apple is reportedly counting on an upgraded version of Siri, with finer control over apps, to make the glasses useful from the start.

## the NWSL

Championship weekend in the NWSL is set, and it came with a serious dose of payback.

Kansas City Current beat Gotham FC two to one on the road Sunday to punch their ticket to the NWSL Championship. The game was played at Icahn Stadium in Manhattan, not Gotham's usual home at Sports Illustrated Stadium, because that field was ruled unfit for play. Croix Bethune, a Midfielder of the Year finalist, put Kansas City ahead in the 20th minute with a direct free kick, only the fourth of its kind in NWSL playoff history. Gotham got back into it after halftime once Rose Lavelle came on, but Haley Hopkins scored a late winner for Kansas City to seal it. The result flips the script from last year, when eighth-seeded Gotham knocked out the Shield-winning Current in the quarterfinals before going on to win the title. This time it's the Current, the fourth seed, eliminating the Shield winners and reigning champions instead.

The other semifinal went to the Washington Spirit, who beat Utah Royals FC two to one at Audi Field. Utah forward Cloé Lacasse scored the fastest goal in NWSL playoff history, just one minute and 57 seconds in, curling a corner kick straight into the net. Washington answered before the eight-minute mark through Trinity Rodman. Utah goalkeeper Mandy McGlynn let a Rodman cross slip under her arm for Washington's equalizer, and a loose pass from Nuria Rábano set up Clara Luvanga for the winner. Utah forward Clara Del Fava said afterward that she felt her team gave the game away rather than lost it fairly. The loss ends Utah's first-ever playoff appearance. For Washington, it's a third straight trip to the NWSL Championship, after losing one to nil to Orlando Pride in 2024 and to Gotham FC in 2025. This time they'll host the final.

Also, the NWSL named its five Most Valuable Player finalists. Reigning MVP Temwa Chawinga is in the running for a third straight award, which would make her the league's first three-time winner. Sophia Wilson could become just the third player ever to win it twice. Golden Boot winner Ashley Sanchez, Trinity Rodman, and Janine Sonis are each chasing their first MVP trophy.
