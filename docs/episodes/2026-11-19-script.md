## Tech and AI

Anthropic's roadshow wrapped with a number well above its original range. Bloomberg reported late Wednesday that the company priced its IPO at 83 dollars a share, above the 70 to 78 dollar range it had floated. That pricing values Anthropic at more than one trillion dollars as shares begin trading on Nasdaq this morning, making it the first publicly traded frontier AI lab.

Nvidia's board has shut the door on a Jensen Huang comeback. Huang resigned as CEO last week amid scrutiny over the company's China dealings, and newly appointed board chair Harvey Jones said the board unanimously accepted that resignation and does not intend to reinstate him. The company is working with recruiting firm Heidrick and Struggles on a permanent replacement. In the meantime, interim CEO and chief financial officer Colette Kress reported quarterly revenue of 115.8 billion dollars, more than double a year earlier, with operating income up 115 percent to 77.5 billion dollars.

Tencent, the Chinese internet giant, says a new US ban on Chinese companies remotely accessing American GPUs will slow it down but won't stop it. Tencent president Martin Lau told investors on the company's earnings call that the restriction will hurt training efficiency for its next generation of models in the short term, but argued it will push China's domestic chip supply chain to mature faster. He said made-in-China chips should still be able to handle the inference workloads that run Tencent's existing AI products.

California regulators have grounded Waymo's freeway robotaxi service nationwide. The state's DMV banned driverless freeway trips after two incidents, including three Waymo vehicles that drove into an active construction zone on Highway 101 near Burlingame, and a separate case where a robotaxi tried to maneuver around highway patrol vehicles. Waymo pulled freeway service in every market it operates, including Los Angeles, Miami, Phoenix and Austin, though it says trained specialists will keep testing on highways while it works to satisfy regulators.

United Launch Alliance says last weekend's Vulcan rocket failure, which cost an Amazon satellite its ride to orbit, traced back to a fuel supply problem rather than the engines themselves. The company said the booster suffered from inadequate fuel delivery to its engines, which were performing normally, causing the rocket to separate stages too early and too slow to reach orbit. A full investigation is continuing under the Federal Aviation Administration.

Microsoft's own AI agent framework became a vector for malware after attackers compromised its official package on the Python Package Index. Security researchers found that versions 1.26.1 and 1.26.2 of Microsoft's agent-framework package, which gets roughly 200,000 downloads a month, were hijacked to install information-stealing malware. One version was reverted, but the attacker then pushed a second compromised version through Microsoft's own publisher account, suggesting the credentials used to publish it still weren't secured.

Separately, researchers flagged a wave of critical bugs in NGINX, the web server software that runs an estimated 225 million websites. F5, which maintains the open-source project, released emergency patches for ten bugs, including remote code execution flaws that could let attackers hijack servers running NGINX with HTTP/2 enabled. One of the flaws had reportedly been sitting undetected in the code for at least a decade. F5 says it has no evidence of active exploitation so far.

Two ticketing and airline companies also disclosed breaches. StubHub International told customers that unauthorized access to its systems between late October and early November may have exposed names, emails, phone numbers and regional settings, though it says passwords and payment details weren't affected. And Ethiopian Airlines said some customer data may have been exposed in a cyberattack on its ShebaMiles frequent-flyer program, a week after Air Botswana disclosed a suspected ransomware incident of its own. Ethiopian Airlines hasn't said when the intrusion happened or how many customers were affected.

In India, Gurugram police have arrested two men, Atar Singh and Lovepreet Singh, over an alleged cyberattack on payments company PayU. Police say attackers broke into three of PayU's cloud servers, manipulated its payout system and pushed through 265 unauthorized transactions across 156 bank accounts, moving a total of about 25.67 crore rupees. Roughly 1.5 crore rupees has been frozen so far while investigators try to determine how the breach happened.

India's National Payments Corporation has also approached Google asking for Android-level protections against UPI fraud, according to the Economic Times. Payment companies want Google to stop malicious apps from exploiting Android permissions to access sensitive data, pointing to malware distributed through sideloaded apps, fake customer support links and spoofed update prompts as the main threat.

And in South Korea, every powered-on phone in the country blared a ten-and-a-half-second emergency alert tone at 8:30 in the morning local time, not because of a disaster, but to catch students trying to sneak smartphones into the country's national college entrance exam. The test bans all technology, so any phone that sounded off in an exam hall gave itself away. Korean media reported the first alert flushed out nine phones, with a second alert planned later in the day to catch any others.

## Women's News

A reader in Akron, Ohio named Robert shared his story with Ms. Magazine this week, part of a collection of accounts from people who came of age during the era of Roe versus Wade. He described driving his partner to a clinic in Maryland in 1984, when she was twenty-three and the two of them were unemployed and unsure their relationship would last. They stayed together. They moved across the country and raised three children. Robert, now seventy-three, said the point of the story isn't whether they'd make the same choice again. It's that they had one. The piece collects similar accounts from readers now in their fifties through their eighties, reflecting on how access to abortion shaped the lives they went on to build.

She says reporting the assault led to her discharge from the Marine Corps. Her account is backed by a military protective order against the man she accused, along with her discharge paperwork and emails from the Marine Corps Special Trial Counsel. The War Horse, which does not name assault survivors without consent, is continuing to report on her case.

And Rewire News Group detailed the safety networks built around queer dance parties in Lagos, Nigeria, where same-sex relationships are illegal. The piece centers on Martins Chukwudera, a community organizer who volunteers as part of what organizers call a welfare brigade at raves like the Group Therapy series. Chukwudera and other volunteers patrol the events, watching for harassment and conflict among women and LGBTQ-plus attendees, in a setting where calling outside authorities for help isn't an option.

## the NFL

Nick Sirianni is staying the course in Philadelphia. The Eagles coach confirmed Joe Milton will start again at quarterback this week, and Sean Mannion will remain the offensive playcaller, despite the unit's struggles this season. Sirianni said the team considered changes during its bye week but decided against anything major. He praised Mannion's preparation and football mind, while acknowledging the offense simply hasn't performed well enough.

The injury report brought some encouraging news for Philadelphia ahead of Sunday's game against the Steelers. DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks both returned to practice on a limited basis after missing multiple games. The Eagles also made a roster move, claiming offensive tackle Ajani Cornelius off waivers from the Dallas Cowboys. ## the NWSL

Ashley Sanchez is the league's Most Valuable Player this season, and the announcement came together with a full slate of NWSL awards handed out on ESPN2 ahead of Saturday's title game. Sanchez, a North Carolina Courage winger, tied the single-season scoring record with 20 goals and added five assists. Her team missed the playoffs entirely, but the performance was enough to beat out Trinity Rodman of the Washington Spirit, Temwa Chawinga of the Kansas City Current, Sophia Wilson of the Portland Thorns, and Janine Sonis of the Denver Summit. Sanchez also claimed the Golden Boot for her 20 goals. Sonis didn't leave empty-handed either, winning Defender of the Year. And Ann-Katrin Berger, the goalkeeper for Shield-winning NJ-NY Gotham FC, took home Goalkeeper of the Year for leading the league's stingiest defense, beating out Jane Campbell of the Houston Dash and Jordan Silkowitz of Bay FC.

That awards show sets up Saturday's NWSL Championship at Audi Field, where the Washington Spirit face the Kansas City Current. Washington got there with a 2-1 win over the Utah Royals. Kansas City punched its ticket with a 2-1 road win over Gotham FC, the team that had just won the Shield.

Meanwhile, Louisville Racing has confirmed it intends to sell the club. The news follows reports that MLS side FC Cincinnati is in talks to buy the team and relocate it by 2028. Racing will finish out the 2027 season in Kentucky, continuing to share Lynn Family Stadium with the USL's Louisville City FC, after posting the league's second-lowest attendance this year and a 7-3-20 record in 2026. FC Cincinnati previously lost out on an NWSL expansion bid, backed by Caitlin Clark, when the league awarded that slot to Denver instead. Recent NWSL expansion fees have climbed sharply, with Atlanta and Columbus paying 165 million dollars and 205 million dollars respectively for their 2028 entries, compared to the under 2 million dollars Soccer Holdings paid for Louisville back in 2021.

Also, the U.S. women's national team has its roster for the upcoming Concacaf W Championship. Head coach Emma Hayes named 23 players for the tournament, which doubles as qualifying for the 2027 World Cup and the 2028 Olympics. Only seven players on the roster have Concacaf W Championship experience, including Emily Fox, Lindsey Heaps, Rose Lavelle, Mallory Swanson, Trinity Rodman, Sophia Wilson, and Ashley Sanchez, all of whom featured in the 2022 edition. Sam Coffey also returns to that mix after missing the last two FIFA windows with injury.

## the WNBA

General manager Monica Wright Rogers described the scene in the team's boardroom as the pick was announced, saying the room broke into celebration once Toronto's name came up ahead of the Houston Comets, who landed the second pick. Wright Rogers called it a landmark moment for the franchise and for Canada. Attention now turns to who Toronto takes with that pick. USC guard JuJu Watkins is the name drawing the most buzz as a projected top choice, though the draft itself is still months away, set for April.
